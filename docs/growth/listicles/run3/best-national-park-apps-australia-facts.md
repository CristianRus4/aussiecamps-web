# Facts: Best national park apps in Australia 2026

All facts checked 2026-10-21 unless stated. "Lookup" = https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=<ID>&country=au. "AU page" = https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id<ID> (prices show "$", AUD). Anything marked **unverified** was not confirmed. Fair tone: never say an app lacks something unless confirmed below. Several agency sites (parks.qld.gov.au, nt.gov.au) return 403 to automated fetches; items from search excerpts are marked "excerpt".

## Key framing facts
- There is no national parks pass or single national park app. Each state and territory runs its own agency, fees and booking system (matches existing guide camping-fees-australia-2026).
- Closures and alerts are published by the agencies. NSW: "People planning a visit to national parks across NSW are urged to check for closures before heading out as conditions can change quickly" (https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/passes-and-fees/fee-collecting-parks). Victoria: "Before visiting any park, check for park closures and stay up to date with warnings and incidents at the VicEmergency website and app ... closures can happen at short notice" (https://www.parks.vic.gov.au/news/2026/01/13/03/43/Fire-affected-parks-remain-closed-as-others-reopen, dated 13 Jan 2026; the page carries a live closures map link). Queensland: "Before you leave home check the park alerts below for the latest information on access, closures and conditions", with RSS feeds and @QldParkAlerts; "158 current park alerts" when read (https://parks.desi.qld.gov.au/park-alerts).
- So the fair line for any third-party app: it is a planning aid, confirm closures and fees with the agency before travel.

### NSW National Parks (official, NPWS)
- id 1213696400; listed seller "Department of Planning, Industry and Environment" (old department name; NPWS now sits under DCCEEW per its site). Free, no IAP on AU page. 4.63 from 2,746 AU ratings; v1.14.0, 2026-05-12; iOS 14+ (lookup). Android: au.gov.nsw.npws.nswparks, Play rating 4.57 from 1,115 (Play page, hl=en_AU&gl=AU). Official page links both stores (https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/app).
- Does: "information on over 225 national parks"; interactive maps; "Download a park when you're at home, then view information and maps during your visit, even without reception or internet access"; find parks, things to do, campgrounds, caravan sites, cabins; check "toilets, picnic areas and other facilities"; favourites; audio tours; booking "internet access required". Account benefits: lists, "Get closure alerts by email", trip intention forms (official page above). Store notes: "Internet coverage is often unavailable in NSW national parks ... download it to your phone". The store text does not mention push closure alerts (unverified in-app).
- Best for: NSW only, free, offline park downloads straight from the agency. Con: NSW only; the app did not list fee amounts in its store text.

### Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park and Kakadu National Park (official, Parks Australia / Director of National Parks)
- Uluru: id 1572928138, free, 5.0 from 5 AU ratings, v5.1.0, 2026-10-13, iOS 15.5+. Kakadu: id 1574290033, free, 5.0 from 3 ratings, v5.1.0, 2026-10-13 (lookups). No IAP on AU pages. Tiny rating bases: say "new, few ratings".
- Store text: free maps and visitor guides; walks, tours, events, itineraries (Kakadu also camping); "Access your favourites offline"; maps "featuring walking tracks and facilities"; "Opening hours, park pass and safety information"; birdwatching with bird calls; "Download the app to your phone before you visit to access information and maps offline."
- Park passes (read directly): Uluru adult (18+) A$38 for 3 days, A$50 annual, under 18 free; the pass is valid for Uluru-Kata Tjuta only (https://parksaustralia.gov.au/uluru/plan/passes/). Kakadu pass valid 7 days; adult (16+) A$40 dry season (15 May to 31 October) and A$25 tropical summer (1 Nov to 14 May); child A$20 and A$12.50; NT residents free; passes bought for 1 July to 31 October 2026 are charged at wet-season rates ("Adult: $25 (usually $40)"); valid for Kakadu only (https://parksaustralia.gov.au/kakadu/plan/passes/). Date-sensitive: write "A$25 in the 2026 wet and reduced season, normally A$40 in the dry season".

## Other candidates

### AussieCamps (ours)
- id 6801098680, Cntxt Limited, iPhone app, v2026.9.6, 2026-09-24, iOS 26+. Price: free to download, with in-app purchases. Rating: not enough ratings to display.
- Data (lib/data/product-facts.json, dataAsOf 2026-09-09): 745 national parks (NSW 267, QLD 195, WA 104, VIC 94, SA 39, NT 23, TAS 23; ACT 0 in our count), 1,838 campgrounds, plus 361 conservation areas, 506 nature reserves, 53 state parks, 41 regional parks, 21 marine parks. Agency sources (lib/data/hubs.meta.json): NSW NPWS, Parks Victoria, Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service, Tasmania Parks and Wildlife Service, DBCA (WA), SA NPWS and DEW, NT Parks and Wildlife, ACT Parks, VIC DEECA recreation sites. Not Parks Australia (Uluru, Kakadu): unverified coverage.
- What a park page shows (App Store text): facilities, fees, rules, access notes, photos, alerts, weather at the spot; Look Around; directions in Apple Maps or Google Maps; trips and saved lists; guides on how national park camping works. Fee and alert detail varies by park (internal research DB: about 95 of 782 national park records had fee entries; do not publish).
- Alerts caveat (verified from code): alerts are captured from agency park pages when the database is built (`Scraper/sources/_australia_common.py` `_alerts`) and ship inside the app; no live alert fetch was found in the app code (only an article reader and photo cache use URLSession). So alerts are a snapshot as of the data date; closures must be checked with the agency. Write that, do not call it a live alert feed.
- Offline: place details and guides work offline; map tiles, routing, weather and photos need signal. No offline map. Best for: iPhone users who want park camping areas across all states beside rest areas, water and dump points. Cons: iPhone only, iOS 26, no traveller reviews, no offline map, not an official agency app.

## Park entry, pass and fee facts (official sources only)
- NSW: motor vehicle entry fees at 45 of 890+ national parks and reserves; fees are per vehicle, walkers and cyclists free; Kosciuszko A$29 per 24 hours from the June long weekend to the October long weekend, A$17 the rest of the year; Garigal A$8 per day at Davidson Park only; most listed parks A$8 per day; day passes bought at pay machines, entry stations, visitor centres, Park'nPay app (https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/passes-and-fees/fee-collecting-parks and /passes-and-fees/day-passes; the fee list is long and varies, quote selectively).
- WA (DBCA): standard vehicle (12 or fewer) A$17, concession A$10; large vehicle A$8 per person; motorcycle A$10; entry fee covers one or more parks the same day; walkers and cyclists exempt; a valid park pass covers it (https://exploreparks.dbca.wa.gov.au/park-entry-fees). Some parks differ (Monkey Mia, Prince Regent, Mitchell River).
- Tasmania: Holiday Pass (all national parks, up to 2 months) A$98.35 per vehicle (up to 8 people), A$49.20 per person on foot, bike or bus; annual pass A$104.75 (concession A$83.85); two-year A$133.80; Cradle Mountain shuttle not included; "Park entry fees are reviewed annually by CPI" (https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/know-before-you-go/park-passes-and-entry-fees). Existing guide notes a new Tasmanian booking system from 3 November 2026.
- South Australia: most parks free to drive in; some charge a vehicle day fee (search excerpts of park pages quote A$14.40; confirm per park); 2-month and 12-month single-park and multi-park (10 parks) passes (https://www.parks.sa.gov.au/book-and-pay/parks-passes, read; amounts not in the text read).
- Victoria: guide camping-fees-australia-2026 says most parks have no entry fee; Parks Victoria's own Wilsons Promontory material says day entry is free (search excerpt, not re-read). Campground fees: half price until July 2027 (existing guide).
- Queensland: no general park entry fee per existing guide FAQ (not re-read this run); camping permit per person or family from 1 July 2026 (au-app-facts.md, excerpt of parks.qld.gov.au); vehicle access permits for sand islands (guide).
- NT: NT Parks Visitor Pass for visitors, residents exempt; excerpt of official page via Tourism NT mirror shows adult A$10 for 1 day, child A$5; other durations ambiguous in the excerpt: unverified (nt.gov.au returned 403). Uluru and Kakadu are separate Parks Australia passes (read above); "All other national parks in the Northern Territory are managed by the Northern Territory Government".
