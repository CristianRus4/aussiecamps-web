# Buying, selling and comparing campervans in Australia: facts file

Read on 2026-10-21. Prices in AUD. Nothing here is legal or financial advice.

## How to read this file

- **[F]** means I fetched the official page directly (WebFetch) and the fact is from its text.
- **[S]** means the fact came from a web-search result summary of the named official page. I did not read the full page. Re-verify before publishing numbers.
- **BLOCKED** means the site returned HTTP 403 or did not load for my scripts (WebFetch and curl). I did not guess those facts.
- Sites blocked or unusable for me: VicRoads (vicroads.vic.gov.au), Service Tasmania and Transport Tasmania (service.tas.gov.au, transport.tas.gov.au), NT Government (nt.gov.au), Access Canberra, sa.gov.au, SIRA NSW, Carsales, Gumtree, Caravan Camping Sales (page fetch), Facebook Marketplace (not attempted, known to block), ppsr.gov.au (timed out on fetch). campertrader.com.au and backpackerscarsales.com.au did not resolve in DNS. backpackercars.com.au redirects to freedomcars.com.au, which lists no vans.

## Part 1. Buying a campervan on a working holiday

### 1.1 The visitor and address problem (the main finding)

- Queensland: a vehicle must be garaged at a Queensland address to register in Queensland [S]. The Queensland transfer page asks for "Proof of Queensland address if you're a new customer or the vehicle won't be kept at your address." [F] https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/transfer/rego (read 2026-10-21)
- Victoria: if you live interstate or overseas you need an interstate or overseas residential or business address AND a Victorian garage address. A bill, bank statement or rates notice, or a signed letter from a Victorian licence holder over 18 confirming the vehicle is garaged at their address, proves it. Overseas passports count as Category A ID. [S] https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registrations/new-registrations/register-vehicle (read 2026-10-21; page BLOCKED to scripts)
- Victoria: with no Victorian licence or VicRoads customer number you must attend a Customer Service Centre with full identity documents [S], same VicRoads source.
- Northern Territory: licensed dealers can register a vehicle for an interstate or overseas resident. The dealer's paperwork needs a copy of the owner's interstate or overseas licence details, and a passport copy for overseas residents, plus form R11 or R47 [S]. https://nt.gov.au/driving/rego/getting-an-nt-registration/licensed-vehicle-dealers-and-registration (listed in search results; page BLOCKED, read 2026-10-21)
- NSW: the transfer page says proof of identity is needed in person. It does not state an address requirement. [F] https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/transfer-a-vehicle-registration (2026-10-21)
- NSW: if you are not already in the system you must attend in person [F] https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driving-nsw/vehicle-registration/how-to-register/registering-a-used-or-secondhand-vehicle (2026-10-21)
- WA: if you have no WA driver licence, include certified copies of proof of identity and age with the MR9 form. The page gives no address rule. [F] https://transport.wa.gov.au/licensing/vehicle/buy-sell-transfer/buy (2026-10-21)
- SA, TAS, ACT: no visitor or address rule confirmed. ACT transfers must be done in person with photo ID and a card [S] https://www.accesscanberra.act.gov.au/driving-transport-and-parking/registration/buying-an-act-registered-vehicle (BLOCKED, 2026-10-21). Tasmania in-person transfer needs a Tasmanian licence or proof of identity [S] https://www.transport.tas.gov.au/registration/buying_selling_or_transferring/transfer_of_registration (BLOCKED, 2026-10-21). Online transfer in Tasmania needs a Tasmanian licence for both parties [S].
- Overseas licence holders: no official page I could read says a foreign licence is refused for registration. NT dealers accept overseas licence details plus passport [S, above]. Not found for other states.
- Overseas-registered vehicles: a vehicle registered overseas and temporarily in Australia cannot be sold in Australia [S]. https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/register/overseas (listed in search; not read, 2026-10-21).

### 1.3 Roadworthy and safety checks on transfer

- NSW: no new pink slip when buying a vehicle with current NSW registration [S]. Most light vehicles over 5 years old need a pink slip at renewal; the report is valid 6 months; some small caravans and trailers exempt [F] https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driving-nsw/vehicle-registration/renewing-vehicle-registration/safety-checks-and-vehicle-inspections (2026-10-21). Pink slip fee: page does not state it (not found). If registration has been cancelled for over 3 months you need a blue slip (AUVIS) plus CTP [F] https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/guide/buying-a-vehicle .
- QLD: seller must give the buyer a current safety certificate (or certificate of inspection) unless exempt; seller can be fined over $700 if not. Fee (1 July 2026): $102.70 for motor vehicles up to 4,500 kg GVM; $51.45 for trailers over 750 kg; valid 2 months or 2,000 km for private sellers (3 months or 1,000 km for dealers). Not required when selling to a dealer. Vehicles with gas fittings also need a gas certificate under 3 months old; caravans and motorhomes are not exempt. [F] https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/safety-certificate and https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/transfer/rego (2026-10-21)
- VIC: seller must supply a roadworthy certificate (RWC) for private sales, issued no more than 30 days before sale, unless exempt; an unregistered vehicle can be sold without one [S]. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/buy-sell-transfer/selling-car/selling-vehicle (BLOCKED). RWC fee: not found.
- WA: inspection at an Authorised Inspection Station is needed to licence an interstate vehicle in WA, and a temporary movement permit [F] https://transport.wa.gov.au/licensing/vehicle/buy-sell-transfer/buy . Whether a licensed WA vehicle needs an inspection on transfer: not stated on the page I read. A lapsed licence needs one [S].
- SA: no roadworthy requirement on transfer found [S] (search found nothing either way; treat as not confirmed).
- TAS: official page says a vehicle never registered, unregistered for 3+ months, or from interstate needs an AIS inspection [S]. No roadworthy on a normal sale (secondary source only).
- NT: roadworthy inspection report "if required" with the application [S]; fee amounts not found. https://nt.gov.au/driving/registration/book-a-vehicle-inspection (BLOCKED).
- ACT: roadworthy inspection before transfer if up to 4.5 t and over 6 years old, or imported [S]. Fee not found.

### 1.4 CTP and registration costs

- NSW: a green slip (CTP) is required to register; trailers and caravans excluded. Light vehicle: 12-month policy from a NSW insurer. A current green slip transfers with the vehicle unless expired or the seller cancelled it and claimed a refund [F/S] https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/guide/buying-a-vehicle ; https://www.sira.nsw.gov.au/motor/compulsory-third-party-motor-vehicle-insurance-green-slips (BLOCKED). Price not found.
- QLD (cost as at 1 October 2026, 4-cylinder light vehicle, 12 months, private use): registration $385.45, traffic improvement fee $67.25, CTP $411.80 to $427.80 depending on insurer, total $864.50 to $880.50 [F] https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/fees/cost (2026-10-21). First-time registrant must nominate a CTP insurer (Suncorp, Allianz, QBE) [S]. Class for campervan not confirmed (MAIC lists motorised homes as Class 2 [S]).
- WA: 2026/27 Motor Injury Insurance for a motor car $520.50, included in the vehicle licence; licence fee is weight-based ($29.76 per 100 kg plus flat fee) [S] https://www.transport.wa.gov.au/licensing/vehicle/license-vehicle/western-australia
- VIC: CTP is the TAC charge built into registration (10% TAC fee mentioned on refunds) [S]. Amount not found.
- SA, TAS, NT, ACT: CTP rules and amounts not confirmed. SA unregistered vehicle needs application for registration plus CTP [S].

### 1.5 Checks before you buy

- PPSR: A vehicle search costs $2.00 online or $7.00 by assisted phone service; fees non-refundable; use the VIN or chassis number. It shows security interests (finance owing), and, via NEVDIS data, written-off and stolen status where available. It does not show amount owing, ownership history, odometer or fines. [S, official ppsr.gov.au pages listed: https://www.ppsr.gov.au/carcheck ; https://ppsr.gov.au/understanding-motor-vehicle-search-results ] (WebFetch timed out; read 2026-10-21 via search summary)
- NSW free registration check shows expiry, status, CTP insurer and expiry, last 3 odometer readings; the paid vehicle history report covers stolen, written-off, operators. [F] https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/guide/buying-a-vehicle
- SA: check vehicle status at Service SA and the PPSR; registration is not proof of ownership [S] https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/driving-and-transport/registration/vehicle-registration/transfers
- REVS (state-run check): REVS is not mentioned on the NSW page [F]. No official REVS page was fetched. Not found.

### 1.6 Dealer vs private (ACCC)

Source: https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/new-and-second-hand-cars and the Dec 2025 report (https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/consumer-issues-second-hand-cars-dec-2025.pdf), read via search summary 2026-10-21 [S].
- Consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law apply to cars from licensed dealers.
- Most used cars from dealers carry a state statutory warranty; reduced for cars over 10 years or over 160,000 km.
- Cooling-off of 1 to 3 clear days is required of dealers in some states; not in WA, NT or TAS; NSW generally none unless bought on finance. Right is usually lost if you take delivery.
- Private sellers: no statutory warranty, no cooling-off, most ACL obligations do not apply; they must still guarantee clear title and no undisclosed security. Run a PPSR search.

### 1.7 Leaving Australia

- Queensland: you can cancel to get a refund of unused registration and the CTP premium, but must surrender the plates. $22.00 administration fee (1 July 2026) is deducted. Traffic improvement fee and other fees are not refunded. [S] https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/cancel
- WA: return plates before selling to be considered for a refund of the remaining licence period; no refund once the vehicle is relicensed in another state; WA licence cannot transfer to a non-WA resident [F] https://transport.wa.gov.au/licensing/vehicle/buy-sell-transfer/sell
- NSW, VIC refund rules: see Part 2.

## Part 2. Selling before you leave

### 2.1 Seller obligations by state

- NSW: lodge a notice of disposal (NOD) straight after sale so the buyer can transfer within 14 days; plates normally stay with the vehicle [F/S] https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/submit-a-notice-of-disposal-for-a-vehicle . Keep proof of sale (Part B on the certificate or a signed receipt) [F]. To cancel instead of sell: take the completed form and plates to a Service NSW Centre; you may get the unused motor vehicle tax back; registration fee and plate fees are not refunded; a cancellation fee is deducted; interstate residents can cancel by post [S] https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/cancel-vehicle-registration-within-nsw . NOD deadline in days: not confirmed on the pages I read.
- VIC: report the sale within 14 days via a Notice of Disposal in myVicRoads; supply an RWC under 30 days old for private sales [S]. Cancelling and refunding unused registration: online refund after $21.50 processing fee and 10% TAC fee [S]. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/buy-sell-transfer/selling-car/selling-vehicle (BLOCKED)
- QLD: seller must supply the safety certificate (see 1.3); complete and keep Part B of form F3520 until the transfer is done; seller pays no transfer fee [F]. Seller stays liable for tolls and infringement notices until transfer. If cancelling: refund of unused rego and CTP premium, plates surrendered, $22.00 fee [S].
- WA: seller must notify within 7 days (penalty if not) via DoTDirect or MR9 seller copy; give buyer the red copy and licence paper. For a refund return the plates before sale; refund runs from the date plates returned. Selling to an interstate buyer: return plates and sell as unlicensed. [F] https://transport.wa.gov.au/licensing/vehicle/buy-sell-transfer/sell
- SA: lodge a notice of disposal within 14 days; online only if the buyer is from SA; interstate buyers need paper NOD (MR96). A Confirmation of Sale letter costs $33 [S] https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/driving-and-transport/registration/vehicle-registration/transfers/lodge-disposal-notice
- TAS: notice of disposal within 7 days [S] https://www.service.tas.gov.au/services/transport/vehicle-registration/dispose-of-a-vehicle-registration/
- NT: notice of disposal (form R06) within 14 days [S] https://nt.gov.au/driving/rego/existing-nt-registration/buying-selling-a-used-vehicle-registration/former-owner-responsibilities-seller
- ACT: seller rules not found.
- CTP refund: QLD (included in the cancellation refund), VIC (TAC in refund), WA (via plates return refund); NSW: green slip does not transfer if the seller cancelled it and claimed a refund [F]. Others not found.

### 2.2 PPSR and finance

- A buyer should run a PPSR search; a private seller must pass clear title and no undisclosed security [S, ACCC]. Seller must clear any finance before sale: no official page fetched stating this as a rule beyond the title guarantee.

### 2.3 Where backpackers sell

Travellers Autobarn blog (read 2026-10-21) [F] https://www.travellers-autobarn.com.au/blog/how-to-sell-your-car-in-australia : fellow backpackers are the best buyers; Gumtree works but needs frequent relisting; Backpacker Cars site; buy-back if bought from them. Keep at least 3 months of rego (advice). A sale at 60% to 80% of purchase price is "pretty good". Quietest April to July in Sydney, Perth, Melbourne; Darwin and Cairns slow December to June. Do not spend 3 to 4 weeks selling. No official source was found for hostel noticeboards or Facebook groups. Depreciation: no official source.

## Part 3. Comparison search demand

### 3.1 What top results say (WebSearch, 2026-10-21)

- "campervan vs caravan Australia": results are blogs and sellers (ytravelblog.com, cubcampers.com.au, latitudefinancial.com.au, 4wdingaustralia.com, Camplify hire pages). Common answer: campervan suits frequent movers and couples (pack up by closing the door); caravan suits long stays and families (leave it set up and drive a car); camper trailer suits rough tracks; caravans cost less upfront but more in fuel and towing vehicle. No government source.
- "powered vs unpowered camping site Australia": results are oztrail.com.au blog, park pages (G'day Parks, Curtin Springs, NT tourism). Common answer: powered gives a mains outlet (fridge, devices, fans), costs more, sits in caravan parks and family campgrounds; unpowered is cheaper, quieter, more remote, needs a power station or solar. Curtin Springs: unpowered sites are first-come, no bookings; powered need advance booking.

### 3.2 Official towing, licence and size facts

- Licence: a car licence covers vehicles up to 4.5 tonne GVM seating up to 12 including driver [S] https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driver-and-rider-licences/licence-classes-and-conditions/classes . A car licence also covers towing a single trailer within combination mass limits (SA page [S] https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/driving-and-transport/licences/licence-details/driver-s-licence-classes). NSW: learners cannot tow; P1 car licence holders can tow up to 250 kg unloaded weight [F] https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driving-nsw/roads-safety-and-rules/vehicle-safety-and-compliance/towing-a-caravan . Other states' provisional rules: not found.
- Brake tiers (NSW) by gross trailer mass: 0 to 750 kg no brakes required; 751 to 2,000 kg brakes on at least one axle; 2,001 to 4,500 kg brakes on all wheels; over 2,000 kg needs a breakaway system; one safety chain under 2,500 kg, two crossed over 2,500 kg [F, same URL].
- Unbraked towing: capped at 750 kg or the manufacturer limit, whichever is lower [S] https://www.transport.wa.gov.au/getmedia/412a9df8-1773-47c4-b29f-51f70f697c7b/DVS_P_Safe_Towing_Guide.pdf . Where no manufacturer limit exists, 1.5 times the towing vehicle's unladen mass with brakes [F, NSW and QLD].
- Speed: single trailer with a vehicle over 4.5 t GVM, or a combination over 4.5 t GCM, maximum 100 km/h [F, NSW]. No general towing speed limit for cars towing under those masses found on the pages read.
- Mirrors: "extra mirrors may be needed" when towing large trailers; no specification given [F, NSW; QLD https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/vehicle-safety/towing/towing-vehicles-and-trailers, read 2026-10-21]. Exact mirror rule: not found.
- Width and length: NSW states total length of vehicle, trailer and load must not exceed 19 m, and load may not make overall width exceed 2.5 m [F, NSW]. NHVR: heavy vehicle width limit 2.5 m and the wider allowance does not apply to trailers; single vehicle length 12.5 m; combination limit 19 m, or 20 m where the trailer has ADR 38 compliant brakes etc. [S] https://www.nhvr.gov.au/road-access/mass-and-dimension/dimension-requirements/length ; https://www.nhvr.gov.au/road-access/mass-and-dimension/dimension-requirements/width . NHVR motorhome page: 12.5 m length and 2.5 m width for motorhomes [S]. Applicability to a car towing a caravan: not confirmed.
- Road trains: NHVR lists a 53.5 m limit for road trains [S]; specific rules for caravans passing road trains: not found.
- ADR: not looked up. Not found.
