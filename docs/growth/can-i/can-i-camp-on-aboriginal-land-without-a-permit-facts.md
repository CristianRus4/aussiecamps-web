# Facts: can-i-camp-on-aboriginal-land-without-a-permit (publishAt 2026-11-15)

Opened 2026-10-21 unless noted. Several land council and NT pages are undated or carry older notices, flagged below.

## Confirmed rules used
- Central Land Council permits (https://www.clc.org.au/permits/, opened; page undated, carries 2020 COVID text): Aboriginal people own more than half the land in the region as inalienable freehold under the Aboriginal Land Rights (NT) Act 1976, private land; permit needed for non-public roads through Aboriginal land, overnight stays in communities, outstations and other Aboriginal land outside larger communities; anyone passing through a community on a public road can visit public areas (store, art centre, council office) without a permit; transit permit needed if the community is reached by a non-public road. Transit permits: Tjukaruru Road (Uluru-Kata Tjuta to the WA border; camping only at Kaltukatjara (Docker River) campground), Gary Junction Road (Papunya to WA border), Sandy Blight Road (separate Ngaanyatjarra permit needed for WA), Glen Helen to Papunya Road (day access for individuals only, Ikuntji Arts Centre, no photography or driving around the community). Transit permits are self-issued online and free; Mereenie Tour Pass free from CLC reception (27 Stuart Highway, Alice Springs), small fee from other outlets (Tourism Central Australia, Ntaria Store, Glen Helen Resort, Kings Creek Station, Kings Canyon Resort); no camping on Mereenie Loop. Entry permits for other non-public roads, including Simpson Desert (Madigan Line, Hay River Track, Colson Track), at least a week to process. Group travellers list all vehicles and passengers on a single application. Alcohol: possession, consumption and sale on Aboriginal land prohibited; transit visitors may carry it if they do not drink it. Photography: private non-commercial needs no media permit; commercial or publishing needs media permit B; do not photograph people without permission. Rare refusals due to ceremony, funerals or wet roads. Not on the transit list: Tanami Road.
- Northern Land Council permits (https://www.nlc.org.au/permits, opened; undated, carries COVID wording): Commonwealth and NT law require a written permit; Aboriginal land is not Crown or public land; permit needed to enter for any purpose, travel by road, visit a community, stay overnight; permits generally cover entry only unless camping, fishing, filming is written in; in most cases no charge but Traditional Owners have set access and camping fees for some locations; allow up to 10 days (at least 10 days in the recreation FAQ); recreation permit covers sites identified by Traditional Owners with rules on numbers and length of stay, some allow camping, stopping on transit routes only in certain locations; closures for funerals and ceremonies at short notice; unauthorised entry fine up to 50 penalty units (Commonwealth) or 8 penalty units (NT) and insurance claims may rely on a valid permit; tour operators need a work permit. The page also says recreation applications are "temporarily unavailable online" (undated); not used on the page.
- WA Government "Apply for a permit to access or travel through Aboriginal land" (https://www.wa.gov.au/government/document-collections/apply-permit-access-or-travel-through-aboriginal-land, opened): Entry Permit required for proclaimed Aboriginal Lands Trust reserves; ALT estate over 8 per cent of WA; transit permits via ALPS, free of charge, usually no more than three days, per vehicle, weekdays 8.30am to 5pm AWST, apply several weeks ahead but not more than 30 days before; Great Central Road NT border to Laverton needs a transit permit; communities may levy charges to enter or camp, native title holders may require a Visitor's Pass; not issued for commercial tourism.
- Ngaanyatjarra Council permits (https://www.ngaanyatjarra.org.au/permits, opened): standard 3-day transit permit, three days and two nights between the NT and Ngaanyatjarraku Shire borders; automatic approval roads: Great Central Road, Ngaanyatjarra Southern Road, Sandy Blight Junction Road, Gunbarrel Highway, Giles Mulga Park Road, Connie Sue Highway, Heather Highway; abandoned section of the Gunbarrel "NOT OPEN TO PUBLIC"; Council does not issue permits for Anne Beadell Highway, Gary Junction Road, Neale Junction or Kiwirrkurra (apply through DPLH); visitors on the Great Central Road not allowed in communities except essential services; NT permits via CLC.
- Shire of Ngaanyatjarraku (https://www.ngaanyatjarraku.wa.gov.au/tourist-information/plan-your-trip/aboriginal-land-permits.aspx, opened): two applications (Council 3-day transit permit and DPLH permit), allow two to three weeks, traditional owners can refuse, area patrolled and fines issued, communities off limits.
- Explore Parks WA Mitchell River NP (https://exploreparks.dbca.wa.gov.au/park/mitchell-river-national-park, opened): Uunguu Visitor Pass required for entry, bought online before arrival (no phone or internet on Wunambal Gaambera Country); DBCA no longer charges park entry but camping fees apply.
- APY (https://anangu.com.au/apy-programs/permits, opened): transit permits NOT issued to tourists/travellers/4WD enthusiasts crossing any part of the lands; entry permits for friends/family, work, media, research; processing 10 to 14 days; apply 30 days before.
- NPWS South Australia Mamungari CP (https://www.parks.sa.gov.au/parks/mamungari-conservation-park, opened): permit from Maralinga Tjarutja Administration for travel in Maralinga Tjarutja Aboriginal Lands and Mamungari CP, allow 4 to 6 weeks; Woomera Prohibited Area Tourist Access Permit for the Anne Beadell; vehicle entry free, camping fees apply.
- Cape York Tourism Guide, Dec 2023 (https://www.napranum.qld.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Final-Cape-York-Tourist-Guide-3-Dec-2023.pdf, opened): get permission before entering Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander lands; "To camp on Aboriginal land you must contact the Land Trust or local Council for permission"; pre-book national park campsites; visitors seeking to access country outside the residential area of Aurukun need permission from Traditional Owners, Cape York Land Council can help (Aurukun not used on the page).
- Parks Australia Kakadu passes (https://parksaustralia.gov.au/kakadu/plan/passes/, opened): 7-day park pass, online; Kakadu only; extra permit for research, filming, overnight bushwalking or camping outside designated areas. Kakadu camping (https://parksaustralia.gov.au/kakadu/stay/camping/, opened): campgrounds in all seven regions. Kakadu FAQ (https://parksaustralia.gov.au/kakadu/faq/, opened): permit to camp outside designated campsites.
- Parks Australia Uluru FAQ (https://parksaustralia.gov.au/uluru/about/faq/, opened): camping not permitted inside the park; campgrounds at Ayers Rock Resort and Curtin Springs; Great Central Road travellers need CLC permits and show them at the entry station.

## NOT CONFIRMED / left out
- Tanami Road: no official statement that it needs no permit; the page says only that it is not on the CLC transit list and to ask the CLC. Land beside it is Aboriginal land (not stated on any opened official page).
- Canning Stock Route, Kimberley Gibb River Road, Pilbara communities (Punmu, Parnngurr), Kalumburu community permit: no current official page found; left out. Mitchell River covered via Explore Parks WA only. Uunguu Visitor Pass price left out (sources conflict, $45 or $50).
- Cape York: no single permit system or Injinoo/Jardine River ferry permit rule confirmed officially; qld.gov.au and parks.qld.gov.au Apudthama page returned 403.
- Maralinga Tjarutja application email and 2017 processing times used only as in the NPWS page. Connie Sue Highway processing time: not stated; Ngaanyatjarra page lists it as automatic approval.
- Fines under CLC: page figures conflict ($2,100 as at 2020 vs "up to $1000"), so none quoted for the CLC region; NLC penalty units quoted without dollar values (NLC page shows $18,200 and $11,100 for the same 50 units in different places).
- Fees for APY ($22 per adult, from a search extract of the application form), left out.

## CONFLICTS
- camping-rules-northern-territory: NLC fine "$18,200" matches the NLC page top block but the NLC page elsewhere states $11,100; no action. Guide says Mereenie Tour Pass is "free from the CLC"; CLC also lists small fees at other outlets (page states both).
- red-centre-way-camping says the Mereenie permit is "inexpensive and available from visitor centres"; the CLC says it is free from CLC reception and a small fee elsewhere.
- camping-rules-western-australia says Entering an Aboriginal reserve needs a permit "issued through the Department of Planning, Lands and Heritage"; true, but the Great Central Road needs a second permit from the Ngaanyatjarra Council for its section (not mentioned in that guide's excerpt).
