# Facts: can-i-fish-without-a-licence-while-camping-in-australia (publishAt 2026-11-12)

All checked 2026-10-21. "Opened" = fetched in full. "Search extract" = official page blocked to automated fetch (HTTP 403 or timeout: pir.sa.gov.au, dpi.nsw.gov.au, vfa.vic.gov.au, act.gov.au, nt.gov.au) and the rule rests on the official-page text returned by a web search for that page.

## Confirmed rules and fees used
- NSW (https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/services/recreational-fishing-licence, opened): NSW Government recreational fishing licence $7 for 3 days, $14 for 1 month, $35 for 1 year, $85 for 3 years. One fee covering fresh and salt water, activities covered (line fishing, spearfishing, bait collecting, hand collecting invertebrates) and exemptions (under 18; private dam of 2 ha or less; adult assisting under-18s) come from NSW DPI guide extracts (search extract, 2025-26 freshwater guide). Page uses only "under 18" exemption.
- Victoria (https://service.vic.gov.au/find-services/outdoor-and-recreation/buy-a-victorian-recreational-fishing-licence, opened): licence needed for recreational fishing in Victorian waters; $10.00 for 3 days, $24.70 for 28 days, $43.30 for 1 year, $117.40 for 3 years (cheaper online); under-18s and people 70 and over can apply for a free fisher card; exempt groups listed. VFA page extract says only online or participating angling shops sell licences.
- Queensland SIPS (https://www.qld.gov.au/recreation/activities/boating-fishing/rec-fishing/dams/stocked-impoundment-permits/buy, opened, updated 28 July 2026): $13.60 a week, $64.58 a year ($46.45 concession); anyone 18 or over needs a permit to line fish in SIPS impoundments; under 18 does not; one permit covers every impoundment. No general licence: DPI SIPS advisory committee record (search extract) says the government does not currently support a recreational fishing licence; qld.gov.au rules pages do not mention one.
- South Australia (https://pir.sa.gov.au/fishing-and-aquaculture/recreational-fishing/rules, search extract): no licence for recreational fishing; reservoir fishing permit via SA Water; fishery subject to size, bag, boat and possession limits. Fees not used (PIRSA and SA Water extracts differ and the pages are old).
- WA DPIRD (https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/individuals/recreational-fishing/get-a-recreational-fishing-licence/, opened): licences listed: boat $40; net $50; south-west freshwater angling $50; rock lobster $50; abalone $50; marron $50; half fee under 16, Seniors Card and some pensioners; 10% discount for multiple activities; valid 12 months from payment; under 16 no south-west freshwater licence. Detail that boat licence covers line, pots and spear and that rowing or paddling needs none is from a DPIRD search extract.
- Tasmania sea (https://fishing.tas.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-sea-fishing-licences, opened): no licence for rod and line in marine waters; licences for rock lobster, abalone, scallop, graball, mullet nets, beach seine, set line; standard $68.60 first licence; licence year 1 November 2026 to 31 October 2027.
- Tasmania inland (https://www.ifs.tas.gov.au/anglers/manage, opened): 2026-27 adult one-season 1-rod $96.00; 48 hours $29.00; 7 days $49.00; 28 days $76.00; under 14 no licence; junior 14 to 17 free.
- NT (https://nt.gov.au/parks/safety-rules/boating-and-fishing-in-parks, search extract; 403): no recreational fishing licence; fishing permits not required in NT parks and reserves, though some parks need access or camping permits; registration or permit for some waters over Aboriginal land (nt.gov.au "Fishing on Aboriginal land", extract); barramundi NT-wide limit five, 55 cm minimum, tighter in Kakadu, Mary River and Daly River zones (extract); Be Crocwise guidance: assume crocodiles in any Top End waterway, stand at least 5 m back from the bank, clean fish at least 50 m from water unless at a designated facility (extracts).
- ACT (https://www.act.gov.au/environment/animals-and-plants/animals/wildlife-management/fish/recreational-fishing-in-the-act, search extract; 403): no licence in public waters; Googong Reservoir follows NSW rules and needs a NSW licence; anglers must follow the Fisheries Act 2000.
- Marine parks: NSW DPI (https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/fishing/marine-protected-areas, search extract): sanctuary zones ban fishing by any method; zoning maps and FishSmart NSW app; Batemans and Jervis Bay maps show some exceptions. Victoria VFA (https://vfa.vic.gov.au/recreational-fishing/recreational-fishing-guide/waters-with-fishing-restrictions/marine-waters-closed-to-recreational-fishing/restricted-areas-marine-national-parks, search extract): marine national parks and sanctuaries are no-take; Can I fish here? app feature. SA Marine Parks (https://www.marineparks.sa.gov.au/enjoy/fishing/fishing-restrictions, opened): sanctuary zones are no-take, maps and GPS coordinates online, formal warning first offence, $315 expiation second, maximum $100,000 or two years' imprisonment for serious or repeat offenders; PIRSA app shows where you can fish. WA (https://exploreparks.dbca.wa.gov.au/activity/fishing, opened): fishing not permitted in marine nature reserves or sanctuary zones (not used in page body).

## NOT CONFIRMED / left out
- SA reservoir permit cost and reservoir list (PIRSA and SA Water extracts differ).
- Qld size and bag limits apart from the general statement (barramundi 58 to 120 cm, 5 per person is on the qld.gov.au tidal limits page, opened, but not used); Queensland and Great Barrier Reef green-zone rules and maps (GBRMPA extract only) left out of the page.
- WA shore line fishing in the sea is not on DPIRD's licence list; the page words it as "not listed", not as an explicit "no licence needed" statement.
- NSW exemptions beyond under-18 and private dams (pensioner and Aboriginal exemptions differ between DPI guides), Vic exemptions beyond age (concession cards listed on the page, not used).
- Fine amounts for fishing without a licence in each state.
- Tasmania and NT marine reserves, ACT penalties.

## CONFLICTS
- Brief said ACT needs a licence; the ACT Government page (extract) says no licence is needed in public waters, so the page says no licence, with Googong Reservoir needing a NSW licence.
- camping-rules-northern-territory says "Recreational fishing has bag and size limits, and some barramundi waters close for part of the year"; consistent with the extracts, not re-confirmed.
- camping-rules-new-south-wales says marine park sanctuary zones ban fishing; consistent.
