# Facts: can-i-drive-on-the-beach-in-australia (publishAt 2026-11-08)

All pages opened 2026-10-21. These return HTTP 403 to automated fetches (WebFetch and curl): parks.qld.gov.au and mirrors, sixt.com, nt.gov.au, broome.wa.gov.au (curl only; the Shire pages were read through WebFetch). Rows marked "search extract" rest on the official-page text a web search returned for that URL.

## Confirmed rules used
- Qld K'gari (https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/kgari-fraser/camping/camping-permits-and-requirements, search extract): vehicle access permit needed for every vehicle entering K'gari; copy (digital or printed) shown to an authorised officer on request; high-clearance 4WD with low-range gearing; normal Queensland road rules apply on beaches.
- Qld K'gari staying safe and conditions report (https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/kgari-fraser/visiting-safely/staying-safe, search extract): 80 km/h on the eastern beach (maximum), 30 km/h on inland roads; safe speed may be lower than the signed limit; best beach driving around low tide, avoid the two hours either side of high tide; only high-clearance 4WD suitable, AWD not recommended.
- Qld driving on sand (https://parks.qld.gov.au/before-you-visit/visit-with-care/four-wheel-drive-with-care/driving-on-sand, search extract): SUV and AWD vehicles not suitable in Queensland's sand parks; reduce tyre pressure on soft sand within manufacturer specifications and re-inflate before harder surfaces or sealed roads; stay on firm sand, keep off dunes.
- NSW Worimi Conservation Lands (https://worimiconservationlands.com/beach-driving/, opened; NPWS page https://www.nationalparks.nsw.gov.au/visit-a-park/parks/worimi-national-park opened and says annual NSW National Park Passes are not valid, visitors need a beach vehicle permit): 4WD; all vehicles registered with a WCL beach vehicle permit; fines for no valid permit; drive on the waterfront only, no driving or parking on the frontal dune, grass or vegetation; entry free for walkers, cyclists and horse riders; guided tours listed on the site. Distances (19 km and 22 km) differ inside the page, so none is quoted. The page's permit prices were not used.
- NSW MidCoast Council (https://www.midcoast.nsw.gov.au/Community/Sport-and-recreation/Beaches-and-public-pools/Driving-on-beaches, opened): permit displayed on the windscreen; NPWS passes do not allow beach driving; 40 km/h maximum, 15 km/h within 100 metres of other vehicles and beach users; drive between the tide marks; "high tides leave no room for error"; do not drive on vegetation, dunes or nesting areas. Permit fees were not used.
- Vic Parks Victoria media release 28 March 2022 (https://www.parks.vic.gov.au/media-releases/2022/03/28/04/37/fishers-prosecuted-for-driving-on-beaches, opened): "Off-road access to marine and coastal Crown land and beaches by private vehicles is prohibited in accordance with the Land Conservation (Vehicle Control) Act 1972 and Land Conservation (Vehicle Control) Regulations 2013, except where specified exemptions apply"; six commercial fishermen prosecuted over driving on Discovery Bay beach; Steven Fowler guilty of 22 charges, fined $3,000 plus $6,450 costs.
- SA Department for Environment and Water (https://www.environment.sa.gov.au/our-places/beach-driving, opened): no intention to ban all vehicles on all SA beaches; Wauraltee Beach bars large camping vehicles and overnight camping, daytime access for other vehicles remains; Thidna Beach vehicle access limited to a small area north-east of Gleeson's Landing.
- SA Coastal Driving Code of Practice (https://cdn.environment.sa.gov.au/environment/docs/Coastal-Driving-Code-of-Practice.pdf, opened): some beaches do not allow vehicles, have camping limits, allow boat launching only or need a permit; default beach speed limit 40 km/h unless signposted, 25 km/h when passing pedestrians; vehicles registered, drivers licensed, normal road rules; very high tides may leave no hard sand and it is best not to be on the beach; lower tyre pressure; carry gauge, compressor and recovery gear; tell someone where you are going; emergency rescue may be far away and costly.
- WA Shire of Broome Cable Beach ramp page (https://www.broome.wa.gov.au/Shire-Services/Roads-and-Parking/Cable-Beach-Vehicle-Ramp, WebFetch): ramp closed "until mid to late 2026" with no date; 15 km/h speed limit; turtle season 1 to 30 November limited to essential and approved vehicles at night (8pm to 6am) and on days when high tides exceed nine metres, 1 December to 28 February closed 24/7 to all but essential and approved vehicles; penalties up to $200 for disregarding beach closures. Beach rules (road-registered vehicles between the high and low water marks north of the access ramp; not above the high water mark or in dunes) are from a search extract of the Shire's vehicles page, which is now a 404.
- WA Explore Parks WA four wheel driving (https://exploreparks.dbca.wa.gov.au/activity/four-wheel-driving?page=0, opened): stay on designated roads and tracks; seek permits where necessary; off-road vehicle must be registered with the Department of Transport; special considerations apply for coastal national parks and beaches (Code Off Road).
- Tas Arthur-Pieman Recreational Driver Pass (https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/arthur-pieman-conservation-area/apca-recreational-driver-pass, opened): off-road 4WD drivers need the pass; vehicles registered, drivers licensed; drive only on specified tracks, beaches and areas on the track maps; pass shown to PWS or police on request.
- Sixt Australia rental insurance guide (https://www.sixt.com/magazine/tips/car-rental-insurance-in-australia/, search extract; 403 on fetch): driving across beaches and through streams, rivers and floods can exclude you from protection.

## NOT CONFIRMED / left out

- Rainbow Beach / Cooloola / Teewah: extracts say 4WD only, permit needed, closed to vehicles between the first and third cuttings, 40 km/h in the camping area; left out because they rest on mirrored or old pages. Only a table mention of Cooloola recreation area beaches remains.
- Cape York beaches: no driving rules confirmed.
- Treachery Beach (Seal Rocks) and Port Stephens council permits: not confirmed; Seal Rocks No. 1 Beach is "restricted permits only" on the MidCoast page but is not used.
- Eighty Mile Beach vehicle rules: Explore Parks WA marine park material read in extract, no driving wording found.
- Ningaloo driving: Yardie Creek crossing and South Lefroy Bay track notes (extracts only), not used.
- NT beach driving: no statewide rule found (Casuarina Coastal Reserve and Dundee Beach searches returned nothing on vehicles).
- Busselton (WA) off-road vehicle rule: opened, but it concerns vehicles registered as off-road vehicles, so not used.
- Hire-company insurance exclusions for named brands (Apollo, Britz, Hertz, Avis): their terms were not found. A Rainbow Beach 4WD hire company (Adventure Centre) page was read (no sand driving within two and a half hours either side of high tide, none after dark, none on the western beach, K'gari entry via Inskip Point only) but is not on the page; the page uses only the Sixt line.
- Fines: only the Worimi "fines apply", SA $315 plus $102 levy (in the camping guide), Victorian court outcome and Broome $200 closure fine were found; only the Victorian outcome is quoted.

## CONFLICTS
- QPWS pages disagree between sources on K'gari inland speed (30 km/h on QPWS pages, 35 km/h on a tour operator page); 30 km/h used.
- Worimi page says both "more than 19 km" and "over 22 km" of beach; not quoted.
- camping-rules-new-south-wales says "some national park beaches need a park pass"; the Worimi pages say annual park passes are not valid there, consistent with what is on this page.
