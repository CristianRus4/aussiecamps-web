## 1. Route options
1. **Pacific Motorway / Pacific Highway (M1 and A1), coast**
   - OSRM: 978 km, 12 h 47 min (Brisbane, Tweed Heads, Ballina, Grafton, Coffs Harbour, Port Macquarie, Taree, Newcastle, Sydney).
   - Official figure: federal infrastructure investment listing (search extract): "the full 657km of the Pacific Highway between Hexham and the Queensland border is open to traffic as a four-lane divided road as of 17 December 2020". NSW Nationals release (search extract) agrees on December 2020 for the final section (Devils Pulpit to Woodburn). One WSP page says December 2021 for Woolgoolga to Ballina (conflict; use December 2020 and say "completed in December 2020").
   - Wikipedia: 110 km/h on new motorway-standard sections; no current tolls on the highway in NSW; Pacific Motorway Brisbane to Brunswick Heads 150 to 158 km (the article gives both). Do not quote a Sydney-Brisbane total from Wikipedia (it gives 790 and 779 km for the highway proper, which is not CBD to CBD).
   - OSRM legs: Brisbane-Tweed Heads 102 km 1h20; Tweed Heads-Ballina 87 km 1h07; Ballina-Grafton 136 km 1h44; Grafton-Coffs Harbour 85 km 1h07; Coffs-Port Macquarie 154 km 1h55; Port Macquarie-Taree 80 km 1h04; Taree-Newcastle 172 km 2h14; Newcastle-Sydney 161 km 2h12.
2. **New England Highway, inland (via Cunningham Highway, Warwick)**
   - OSRM: 973 km, 13 h 24 min (Brisbane, Warwick, Tenterfield, Glen Innes, Armidale, Tamworth, Scone, Singleton, Sydney). About the same distance as the coast, about 40 min slower by OSRM.
   - Wikipedia: New England Highway is about 880 to 883 km from Yarraman (north of Toowoomba) to Hexham near Newcastle; route follows the Cunningham Highway from Brisbane to Warwick. In 2013-14 the New England and Cunningham highways carried about 13,000 vehicles a day, about half the coastal route (Wikipedia).
   - Road type: not a dual carriageway for most of its length (Wikipedia calls it a National Highway; Transport for NSW and federal releases refer to adding overtaking lanes). Say "mostly single carriageway with overtaking lanes".
   - OSRM legs: Brisbane-Warwick 156 km 2h11; Warwick-Tenterfield 110 km 1h28; Tenterfield-Glen Innes 92 km 1h12; Glen Innes-Armidale 97 km 1h23; Armidale-Tamworth 111 km 1h36; Tamworth-Scone 132 km 1h50; Scone-Singleton 74 km 1h08; Singleton-Sydney 201 km 2h34.
3. **Pacific Highway via Forster (Great Lakes variant): real, optional**
   - OSRM: Taree to Newcastle via Forster and Bulahdelah is 203 km, 2 h 52 min (Taree-Forster 37 km, Forster-Bulahdelah 69 km, Bulahdelah-Newcastle 97 km), against 172 km, 2 h 17 min direct (Taree-Bulahdelah 75 km). So about 31 km and 35 min extra. Name of the lakeside road and any official figure: not source-checked. Mention as a detour only.
   - Corridor data shows the Myall Lakes campgrounds (Mungo Brush, Bombah Point, Korsmans Landing, Boomeri, etc., all "Park entry fee") 8 to 11 km off the main line near Bulahdelah: AU00263, AU00213, AU00067 (see section 2).

Inland: Day 1 Brisbane to Tenterfield about 266 km (156+110); Day 2 Tenterfield to Tamworth about 300 km (92+97+111); Day 3 Tamworth to Sydney about 407 km (132+74+201).

## 4. Road conditions, closures, tolls
- Live sources: Live Traffic NSW (https://www.livetraffic.com, phone 132 701 from the Transport for NSW closure reports), QLDTraffic (https://qldtraffic.qld.gov.au, Department of Transport and Main Roads; alert types include flooding, crashes, roadworks; shows rest areas and truck restrictions). Its phone number was not confirmed as a road line (13 12 30 is public transport).
- Flood closures, coast: March 2022 reports (news, search extract): Pacific Highway closed between Ballina and Grafton, Lismore isolated; Transport for NSW reported the Pacific Motorway "fully open to all traffic as at 4pm on March 6"; later closures between Wardell and Tintenbar and locals-only from Glenugie. Heavy vehicles were detoured inland (Grafton, Casino, Lismore, rejoin north of Ballina); motorists were urged to use the New England Highway. Use as an example of "closures happen after heavy rain", no current closure found for 21 October 2026.
- Snow and ice, inland: Wikipedia (not an authority) says parts of the New England Highway get frost and snow in winter, especially across 350 km from the Moonbi Ranges to Stanthorpe. Official snow-closure rules not found: say "check Live Traffic NSW in winter".
- Works (search extracts): southbound overtaking lane about 30 km south of Tamworth, 1.2 km, due late March 2027, works 6.30am to 6pm Monday to Friday and Saturdays when needed; Armidale safety upgrade (350 m overtaking lane extension) was projected for end of March 2026; Tenterfield heavy vehicle bypass is at development stage only.
- Tolls, Queensland: Qld Government guidance (staging copy of qld.gov.au, search extract): visitors can pay up to 3 days after using a toll road, otherwise the registered owner gets an unpaid toll notice. Linkt Road Pass page: "Road passes can cover trips made up to 5 days ago on all toll roads in Queensland." Linkt one-off page (search extract): $10.53 admin fee unless paid within 9 days. Road names Gateway Motorway, Logan Motorway, Clem7, Go Between Bridge, Legacy Way, AirportlinkM7: only from a rental company PDF (NOT CONFIRMED). Pass cost not found. Dates conflict (3, 5, 9 days): tell readers to pay by the earliest, 3 days.
- Tolls, NSW: NSW Government: "you have 72 hours to pay or create a toll account"; eMU Casual Pass covers up to 30 days (Service NSW). Pacific Highway north of Hexham: no tolls (Wikipedia). Which Sydney tolls you meet depends on your route into the CBD: NOT CONFIRMED by name.

## 5. State border rules (Queensland and NSW)
- **Red imported fire ant (RIFA), not a fruit fly border.**
  - NSW DPIRD (search extract of dpird.nsw.gov.au; page returned 403): anyone moving fire ant carriers from the red, orange or yellow zones on the NSW DPI map must comply with the current Emergency Order. Order numbers seen: No. 4 (2026) on a Glenrac page dated 20 April; No. 9 (2026) published 4 August 2026 after the Currumbin Valley detection; a plain-English guide for No. 10 (2026). Say "the current Biosecurity (Fire Ant) Emergency Order" and link NSW DPIRD, never a number.
  - One search extract: the Emergency Order "has been extended for six months to 27 January 2027". Ambiguous which order; do not print the date as a fact without checking.
  - Controlled movement (Glenrac): the Order covers "all persons who carry out a controlled movement of fire ant carriers into NSW", meaning movement from a Queensland or NSW fire ant infested area into any part of NSW, and from the NSW movement control areas of Murwillumbah and Wardell.
  - Carriers named by DPIRD (search extract): heavy vehicles, agricultural and horticultural equipment, earth moving equipment, pallets, packaging, containers and covers, building waste, green waste, mulch, soil, compost and manure, growing media, soil samples, hay. Caravans, campervans and camping gear are not in that list and no source says they are exempt: say "the rules are written for carrier materials; check with NSW DPIRD (1800 680 244) or Biosecurity Queensland (13 25 23) about anything you carry".
  - Queensland (Moreton Bay council extract): carriers are soil, manure, hay, straw, sugarcane, products of mining, quarrying or excavation, plants and their pots and by-products such as mulch, sawdust and compost. Queensland's Biosecurity Regulation 2016 prescribes the procedures. Report suspect ants: 13 22 68 (business.qld.gov.au page, last updated 15 May 2026). Zones are reviewed monthly.
  - Enforcement: Operation VICTA, roadside checks across northern NSW; vehicles moving materials in breach "are directed to return to Queensland" and penalty notices are issued (NSW DPIRD extract). Infested area about 1,111,800 ha of south east Queensland and parts of northern NSW (extract).
  - New: fire ant nest at Enfield Intermodal Logistics Centre, Sydney inner west. Treatment 19 October to 30 November 2026 in publicly accessible areas within 2 km; a Biosecurity Control Order to take effect 26 October 2026 per one extract and "end of October" per another (conflict). Mention only as "a detection in Sydney's inner west in October 2026; check NSW DPIRD for the control order".
- **Plant zones (Interstate Quarantine site, undated):** NSW Banana Bunchy Top Virus Control Zone covers Tweed, Lismore, Ballina and Byron: "Do not move banana plants, bells, pseudo stems or leaves out of this area." The Pacific route crosses it. Queensland: "The whole of the state is a biosecurity zone for bananas, grape plants, mangoes and sugarcane. Do not take any plants, soil or items that have been in contact with these plants into Queensland." Melon thrips South East Queensland Restricted Area is listed (plants). NSW Citrus Red Mite Biosecurity Zone covers the Central Coast and Sydney metropolitan homes ("Do not move host plants including citrus plants out of this area").
- Fruit fly: no Queensland-NSW travellers' fruit rule was found. Do not say there is none.
- There are no border checkpoints confirmed on either route; Operation VICTA is roadside checks, not a fixed station.
