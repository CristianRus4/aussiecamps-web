## 1. Route options

**A. Pacific Motorway M1 direct, Brisbane to Gold Coast, Tweed Heads, Byron Bay**
- OSRM: 173 km, 2h27 (Brisbane to Gold Coast 76 km, to Tweed Heads 30 km, to Byron Bay 66 km). [1]
- Travel-guide figures (Google Maps based, not official): about 166 km and 1h52 from Brisbane CBD, 174 km from Brisbane Airport. No Tourism and Events Queensland or Destination NSW figure found. Use "about 165 to 175 km" and cite OSRM. [2]
- The Pacific Motorway is the Queensland M1 (exit 66 signs Smith Street Motorway to Southport and Pacific Motorway M1 to Surfers Paradise and Coolangatta, per a Commons photo of the sign).

**B. Gold Coast hinterland: Mount Tamborine, Springbrook, Murwillumbah, Byron Bay**
- OSRM: 237 km, 4h10 (Brisbane to Tamborine 72 km, to Springbrook 55, to Murwillumbah 59, to Byron Bay 51). [1]
- Lamington National Park: Green Mountains (O'Reilly's) from the M1 at Nerang via Canungra and Kidston Street; Binna Burra from the M1 at Nerang via Beechmont. The road to Green Mountains is "sealed but narrow, winding, and has many blind corners" (trail guide, search extract; not an authority). [3]
- Springbrook and Natural Bridge are in Springbrook National Park (Gold Coast). The Gold Coast-Springbrook Road reopened after 2022 landslip reconstruction (Queensland Reconstruction Authority case study, search extract; date not confirmed). [4]

**C. Inland via Beaudesert, Rathdowney, Woodenbong, Kyogle, Lismore to Byron Bay**
- OSRM: 339 km, 5h58 (Beaudesert 70 km, Rathdowney 31, Kyogle 112, Lismore 81, Byron Bay 45). [1]
- OSRM's Rathdowney to Kyogle leg (112 km, 2h25) follows the Mount Lindesay Highway and Summerland Way, not Lions Road. [1][5]
- Mount Lindesay Highway runs from Brisbane to the NSW border where it meets the Summerland Way (Wikipedia). [5]
- Lions Road (Rathdowney to Wiangaree, about 45 km, a sealed scenic shortcut across the border ranges per a rider site) is not a route to recommend: Kyogle Council works update of 2 April 2026 (search extract) says it "remains closed from Murphy Standfield Bridge to the border"; an undated council notice says it is open to light traffic only, is "not a main road", must not be used by caravans or heavy traffic, and will close again for further landslip repairs. Status NOT CONFIRMED: check Live Traffic NSW. [6]
- Mount Warning (Wollumbin) is reached from Murwillumbah by Mount Warning Road; see section 7.

**D. Gold Coast Highway coast route**: through Surfers Paradise, Burleigh, Coolangatta/Tweed Heads. No separate OSRM run; no source on distance. Do not give a km figure.

## 2. Legs and overnight stops
This is a one-day drive on route A. Suggested splits, OSRM:
- Route B over two days: Brisbane to Mount Tamborine 72 km (1h06); Tamborine to Springbrook 55 km (1h11); Springbrook to Murwillumbah 59 km (1h03); Murwillumbah to Byron Bay 51 km (0h48). [1]
- Route C over two days: Brisbane to Beaudesert 70 km (1h04) to Rathdowney 31 km (0h25) to Kyogle 112 km (2h25) to Lismore 81 km (1h18) to Byron Bay 45 km (0h45). [1]

**Beaudesert**: Beaudesert Caravan & Tourist Park, 57-63 Albert Street (Queensland tourism and Visit Brisbane listings, search extract): cabins, powered and unpowered sites, camp kitchen, laundry, dump point. [7]
**Murwillumbah / Tweed Valley**: Greenhills Caravan Park, 488 Tweed Valley Way, South Murwillumbah (directory listing, 48 powered sites); Mt Warning Rainforest Park, 153 Mt Warning Road (travel listing). Confirm on operator sites. [8]

[9]

## 5. State border rules
**Red imported fire ants (Queensland and northern NSW)**
- Queensland biosecurity zones were updated effective 15 April 2026 in six council areas: Logan, Gold Coast, Moreton Bay, Lockyer Valley, Scenic Rim and Sunshine Coast. The zones extend 5 km beyond detections. Carriers: baled hay or straw, manure, mulch, potted plants, quarry and mining materials, soil, turf. "Fire ants can hitch a ride in equipment or materials that have been in contact with soil or stored outdoors." The article does not set out the movement rules (finia.org.au, 10 May 2026, fetched; it summarises Queensland's zones). [19]
- Queensland's interstate plant quarantine zone map (last updated 5 August 2026) is the reference for movement out of Queensland; check the destination state's rules first (search extracts). Queensland hotline 13 22 68 (search extract). [20]
- NSW: movement of fire ant carriers from the infested areas is governed by the Biosecurity (Fire Ant) Emergency Order. NSW DPI (search extract): "no detections of fire ants in NSW since July 2025" and the infested area is about 1,111,800 hectares across south-east Queensland and parts of northern NSW. Order No. 10 of 2026 (plain-English guide, search extract) sets three zones: a Queensland infested area, a NSW infested area along the border near Tweed Heads South, and NSW movement control areas within 5 km of the Murwillumbah and Wardell infestations. The NSW DPI pages returned 403. [21]
- NSW Government, 8 March 2024 (fetched): Piggabeen and Cobaki were "classified as a fire ant infested area" after a detection at Currumbin Waters about 1.2 km from the border; moving soil, mulch, turf and hay out of that NSW area "may require a Record of Movement Declaration and a Plant Health Certificate". NSW report line: 1800 680 244. This is a 2024 release; current boundaries NOT CONFIRMED. [22]
- NSW DPI (search extract): turf from the Queensland infested area must be treated at the point of lay, and a group hay permit expired on 2 June 2025.
- Conflict: a Tweed Heads detection on 2 July 2025 and a February 2026 news item about Murwillumbah appear in search results next to the DPI claim of none since July 2025. Do not state a date for the last NSW detection. [21][23]
- No source found that says ordinary visitors' cars, caravans, camping gear or firewood are controlled items. Say only what the carrier list says; do not add. NOT CONFIRMED.
- The Interstate Quarantine page does not mention fire ants; it lists Queensland as having no fruit fly zone and a statewide zone for bananas, grape plants, mangoes and sugarcane ("Do not take any plants, soil or items that have been in contact with these plants into Queensland"). It lists no fruit fly restrictions for NSW. [24]

## 7. Other facts a driver needs
- Time zone: NSW daylight saving began Sunday 4 October 2026 at 2am AEST and ends Sunday 4 April 2027 at 3am AEDT. "Queensland, Western Australia, and the Northern Territory do not" observe it (NSW Government, fetched). [25] Wentworth Council repeats the 4 October date. Queensland stays on standard time all year; so from 4 October 2026 to 4 April 2027 the NSW side of the border is one hour ahead of Queensland (AEDT UTC+11 against AEST UTC+10, arithmetic from those two statements). Driving south, clocks go forward one hour at Tweed Heads/Coolangatta. A Karmactive article says Coolangatta and Tweed Heads are split by that hour until 4 April 2027 (search extract). [25][26]
- Mount Warning (Wollumbin): NPWS local alert pages (search extract; start date 30 June 2023, no end date) say the summit track and summit are closed until further notice; Korrumbyn Creek picnic area, Lyrebird walking track and visitor facilities at Breakfast Creek remain open; penalties apply. The newest review date shown was 13 August 2026 (on a related page); NOT CONFIRMED against the Wollumbin alert itself (the page text did not load). Byron NPWS office 02 6639 8300. [27]
- QPWS camping bookings:
  - Lamington: O'Reilly's Campground (Green Mountains) is privately managed, with tent, campervan and safari tent sites; a privately managed campground sits beside Binna Burra; remote bush camps need a permit booked online ahead for weekends and school or public holidays (QPWS page, search extract). Binna Burra Lodge phone (07) 5533 3622 (QPWS page, search extract). [13]
  - Springbrook: The Settlement camping area is QPWS; "you must book campsites in advance"; a tag with the booking number must be displayed; search extract also says a maximum of two consecutive nights and no more than eight people per group (from a snippet; not confirmed on the QPWS page). [28]
  - Mount Barney: "camping areas can only be reached on foot... no facilities"; "To camp in the national park you will need a camping permit (fees apply). It is recommended that you book online 6 to 8 weeks in advance for public holidays and 3 to 6 weeks in advance during the rest of the year." [15]
  - The QPWS pages returned 403 to scripts. Fees NOT CONFIRMED (a directory's $5.75 per person was unverified).
- Park fees on route: Queensland national parks charge no entry fee; not sourced here. Do not state.
