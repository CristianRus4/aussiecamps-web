## 1. Route options
1. **Main route: Bruce Highway, Flinders Highway (Townsville, Charters Towers, Cloncurry), Barkly Highway (Mount Isa, Camooweal, Barkly Homestead), Stuart Highway north (Threeways, Tennant Creek, Daly Waters, Katherine) to Darwin.** All sealed.
   - OSRM: 2,911 km, 33 h 16 min (Cairns, Townsville, Charters Towers, Cloncurry, Mount Isa, Camooweal, Barkly Homestead, Tennant Creek, Daly Waters, Katherine, Darwin). Say "about 2,900 km".
   - Legs (OSRM): Cairns-Townsville 347 km 4h43; Townsville-Charters Towers 136 km 1h41; Charters Towers-Cloncurry 649 km 7h55; Cloncurry-Mount Isa 121 km 1h34; Mount Isa-Camooweal 191 km 2h16; Camooweal-Barkly Homestead 250 km 2h27; Barkly Homestead-Tennant Creek 222 km 2h11; Tennant Creek-Daly Waters 406 km 4h05; Daly Waters-Katherine 276 km 2h55; Katherine-Darwin 315 km 3h25.
   - Cross-checks: Wikipedia Barkly Highway is 754 km Cloncurry to Threeways, "the only sealed road between Queensland and the Northern Territory"; Camooweal to Tennant Creek about 471 km and Mount Isa to Tennant Creek about 661 km (search extracts, third-party). Tourism NT: Katherine to Darwin 316 km, Tennant Creek to Daly Waters about 400 km (Explorers Way itinerary).
2. **Inland Gulf variant: Cairns, Mareeba, Mount Surprise, Georgetown, Croydon, Normanton, then Burke Developmental Road to Cloncurry, then as route 1.**
   - OSRM: 2,870 km, 32 h 40 min (via Mareeba, Mt Surprise, Georgetown, Croydon, Normanton, Cloncurry). About 40 km shorter than route 1 by OSRM.
   - Legs (OSRM): Cairns-Mareeba 63 km 1h01; Mareeba-Mt Surprise 252 km 3h21; Mt Surprise-Georgetown 93 km 1h10; Georgetown-Croydon 148 km 1h51; Croydon-Normanton 154 km 1h52; Normanton-Cloncurry 380 km 4h26.
   - Road type: Savannah Way visitor guide (2019, Gulf Savannah) says Ravenshoe, Mt Garnet, Mt Surprise, Georgetown, Croydon, Normanton and Karumba is "sealed and suitable for caravan and two wheel drive touring" (search extract). Whether the Normanton to Cloncurry section is fully sealed now: NOT CONFIRMED.
3. **Savannah Way: Cairns, Mareeba, Georgetown, Croydon, Normanton, Burketown, Hells Gate Roadhouse, Borroloola, then Carpentaria Highway to the Stuart Highway (Daly Waters) or the Roper Bar route, Katherine, Darwin.** Partly unsealed.
   - OSRM direct: 2,428 km (Cairns, Georgetown, Normanton, Burketown, Borroloola, Katherine, Darwin); Cairns to Katherine 2,113 km. Say "about 2,400 to 2,500 km direct".
   - Savannah Way (official tourism body, savannahway.com.au): "Estimated Travel Distance: 3,530.7 kms" Cairns to Darwin for the longer tour via Undara, Karumba, Boodjamulla, Daly Waters and Kakadu, "Estimated Travel Period: 17 Days including Kakadu National Park", and "allow at least two weeks or more". The gap to OSRM is the detours (Karumba, Boodjamulla, Kakadu). Do not set both side by side as the same route.
   - Road type: same page: "The route makes use of sealed and unsealed roads"; "Sealed roads from Borroloola onwards"; the 2019 visitor guide says roads from Normanton to Burketown and beyond are largely unsealed and recommended for four wheel drive (older, search extract). Third-party guides: Burketown to Borroloola is gravel with dips and water crossings, Borroloola east to Normanton "roughly 630 km and nearly all unsealed" (Caravan World, search extract). The same Savannah Way page says a 4WD is needed.
   - OSRM legs, distance only: Normanton-Burketown 253 km; Burketown-Borroloola 491 km (via Hells Gate); Borroloola-Katherine 660 km. Third-party: Burketown to Doomadgee 127 km, to Hells Gate Roadhouse a further 81 km.
4. **Gulf Developmental Road / Burke Developmental Road** are the Qld roads in options 2 and 3 (Georgetown to Normanton is the Gulf Developmental Road). Wikipedia lists the Gulf Developmental Road: do not give a separate figure.
- Route 1 is the only one a caravan or any 2WD can do without checking road status. Route 3 needs 4WD or a high-clearance vehicle per the sources above; say "check each section before you go".

## 2. Legs and overnight stops (route 1, suggested)

## 4. Road conditions and seasonal closures
- Live sources: QLDTraffic (qldtraffic.qld.gov.au, TMR; shows alerts including flooding, cameras, heavy vehicle rest areas; phone not confirmed) and Road Report NT (roadreport.nt.gov.au or 1800 246 199, Tourism NT). Carpentaria Shire Council publishes a weekly road report (carpentaria.qld.gov.au) and the Roper Gulf Regional Council publishes NT closure notices (ropergulf.nt.gov.au).
- Wet season: Tourism NT: from November to April "some roads may be closed due to flooding"; do not drive on closed roads. Savannah Way: "Sections of the route are closed during the wet season (December to March)"; access in April and May "depends on rainfall easing"; best "May to October". Say "roughly November to April" and name both sources.
- 2026 examples (QLDTraffic Far North listings and Carpentaria Shire weekly reports, search extracts, historical): in March 2026 the Gulf Developmental Road between Normanton and the Croydon Shire boundary was closed to all traffic for long-term flooding; in February to April 2026 sections of the Burke Developmental Road and the Normanton to Burketown road were listed closed, and by April some were "open with caution". No current closure was found for 21 October 2026. Do not claim any road is open or closed today.
- NT closures (search extracts, historical): Roper Highway and Nathan River Road closed in past wet seasons (Roper Gulf notice, Feb 2021); the Stuart Highway north of Threeways closed for days in past floods; NT flood study: Stuart Highway closed 100 times between Oct 2005 and Mar 2024, closures averaging about 35 hours.
- Speed limits: Tourism NT: "The open-road limit is 110 km/h unless signed otherwise, and certain highways allow up to 130 km/h." Wikipedia: Barkly Highway NT section has 130 km/h along most of its length. Safe wording: "up to 130 km/h where signed in the NT; the sign decides". Queensland limit not sourced.
- Road trains (NT Government, search extract): can be over 50 m with up to three trailers; overtaking takes up to 2.5 km at 100 km/h; need 1 km of clear road ahead. Link driving-with-road-trains-australia.
- Wildlife (Tourism NT): many NT roads are unfenced; animals most at dawn, dusk and night; "Never swerve to avoid animals".
- Tolls: none on this route (not sourced; omit).
- Unsealed roads: Tourism NT: "four-wheel drive (4WD) vehicles are best for dirt roads"; unsealed conditions can change quickly in wet weather or with large numbers of road trains and cattle trucks (Savannah Way visitor guide, search extract).

## 5. Border and biosecurity rules
- No Queensland or NT checkpoint was found on either border crossing. Say "we found no checkpoint". SBS (third-party search extract): NT borders are not patrolled; quarantine bins at NT airports and railway stations.
- Interstate Quarantine (interstatequarantine.org.au, undated, opened 2026-10-21), Queensland: "The whole of the state is a biosecurity zone for bananas, grape plants, mangoes and sugarcane. Do not take any plants, soil or items that have been in contact with these plants into Queensland." The page lists zones including: Melon Thrips North Queensland Restricted Area ("areas inland and south of Cooktown, including Mossman, Cairns, Innisfail and Townville, down to Bowen"), Cercospora Leaf Spot Affected Area (in the Mareeba Shire, west of Cairns), Cape York Peninsula Pest Biosecurity Zones, Electric Ant Biosecurity Zones, Banana Pest Biosecurity Zones, Fire Ant Pest Biosecurity Zone; contact Queensland DAF on 13 25 23 or info@daf.qld.gov.au. It does not mention fruit fly in Queensland. Say what leaving Cairns means is NOT CONFIRMED: no source says what a driver may carry out of the Cairns zones.
- NT entering (NT Government plants-and-quarantine pages, search extract; page 403): restrictions cover fruit and vegetables and the plants that produce them, seed and grain, soil, compost and potting mix, plant packaging and farm or earthmoving equipment; the fruit flies of concern are Mediterranean fruit fly, cucumber fruit fly and banana fly; "uncertified bananas from North Queensland are not allowed into the NT"; the only mango varieties that do not need treatment are Kensington Pride, Calypso and Honey Gold; import notices for polyphagous shot-hole borer and tomato brown rugose fruit virus (affects tomato, capsicum and chillies). The NT page says if unsure, check the Interstate Quarantine site or contact Plant Biosecurity. Writer: leave a line "no checkpoint found, but the rules apply; check NT Plant Biosecurity" and list only items quoted above.
- Interstate Quarantine NT zones on the Stuart Highway: the "Adelaide River" melon thrips rule applies to produce carried south beyond Adelaide River (not northbound to Darwin); the Ti Tree Fruit Fly Exclusion Zone (Tennant Creek to Alice Springs) is not on this route unless the reader goes on to Alice Springs.

## 7. Other facts a driver needs
- Time zone (secondary extracts only; no official page opened): Queensland is on Australian Eastern Standard Time (AEST, UTC+10) all year and does not observe daylight saving; the NT is on Australian Central Standard Time (ACST, UTC+9:30) with no daylight saving. So the NT is 30 minutes behind Queensland all year, and clocks go back 30 minutes when you cross the border at Camooweal (Barkly Highway) or Hells Gate/Borroloola (Savannah Way). Camooweal is listed as AEST (Wikipedia, extract). No source opened shows signs or clocks at the crossing. Mark "30 minutes" as secondary-source based.
- National parks a driver stops at:
  - **Boodjamulla (Lawn Hill)** (Queensland Parks, search extract of parks.des.qld.gov.au camping page): camping permits are required and fees apply; pre-paid booking is essential for March to October; outside that, last-minute bookings and payment at Adels Grove are possible only "subject to site availability and wet season closures"; permits are not sold inside the park. Visiting-safely page: "during the wet season (October-April) it is recommended that visitors travel by four-wheel-drive and carry an over-supply of food"; rising creeks can cut roads and leave visitors stranded for days. Access from Gregory Downs is unsealed for 100 km. Adels Grove is a private camping park about 10 km from the gorge.
  - **Limmen National Park (NT)** (Tourism NT page, fetched): "Purchase an NT Parks Visitor Pass before you arrive" (NT residents exempt); "all campsites must be booked online before your visit"; Western Lost City is "a 28 km 4WD track" and needs a key from the Nathan River Ranger Station; no fuel between Roper Bar and Cape Crawford (338 km). Limmen River campground is 4WD only with 5 designated sites (third-party extract, NOT CONFIRMED by NT Parks).
  - **Elsey National Park, Mataranka** (NT Parks, search extract): NT Parks Visitor Pass required; thermal waters 34 C year round; contact (08) 8973 8888.
- Crocodiles:
  - Queensland (Qld Government crocodile management plan, search extract): estuarine crocodiles occur throughout the Gulf of Carpentaria and Cape York; "there is a possibility that crocodiles may be present in any water body north of Gladstone"; obey warning signs and never swim in water where crocodiles may live even without a sign. Report sightings to CrocWatch on 1300 130 372. Council or park-specific sign locations at Karumba and Normanton: NOT CONFIRMED.
  - NT (NT Government Be Crocwise, search extract): swim only where a swimming area sign is displayed; "If a waterway does not have a swimming area sign, do not swim in this area"; the government swimming table lists the Mataranka thermal pool and Bitter Springs as open all year except extreme flooding; camp at least 50 m from the water's edge; land-based anglers stand 5 m back; report sightings to 0419 822 859.
- Link: wet-season-camping-top-end-northern-wa (NT wet season), queensland-outback-way.
