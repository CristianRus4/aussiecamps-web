# Darwin to Alice Springs drive: facts (slug darwin-to-alice-springs-drive)

Researched 2026-10-21. OSRM = "OSRM routing on OpenStreetMap data, computed 21 October 2026" (car, no stops, no caravan, CBD to CBD). Most nt.gov.au pages returned 403 to scripts, so NT Government wording below is from search extracts of the official page (marked "search extract"). Say "about" on every figure. Route is one road, the Stuart Highway (Wikipedia: route number changes from A1 to A87 at Daly Waters). ## 1. Route options
Only one realistic sealed route: **Stuart Highway** (Explorers Way), Darwin, Adelaide River, Pine Creek, Katherine, Mataranka, Daly Waters, Elliott, Tennant Creek, Devils Marbles, Barrow Creek, Ti Tree, Alice Springs.
- OSRM: 1,525 km, 16 h 26 min driving (Darwin, Adelaide River, Katherine, Mataranka, Daly Waters, Elliott, Tennant Creek, Devils Marbles, Barrow Creek, Alice Springs). The AussieCamps corridor tool measured 1,529 km along the same line.
- Wikipedia (secondary): Darwin to Alice Springs 1,499 km (junction table), sums of text segments 1,498 km. Gap to OSRM is the CBD start/end. Safe wording: "about 1,500 km".
- Tourism NT itineraries (official, Explorers Way pages): Katherine to Darwin 316 km; Daly Waters to Mataranka 167 km; Mataranka to Katherine 106 km; Tennant Creek to Daly Waters about 400 km (a trade page says 394 km, about four hours); Alice Springs to Tennant Creek 510 km. These agree with OSRM within a few km, except Tennant Creek to Daly Waters (OSRM 409 km via Elliott).
- Road type: Wikipedia says dual carriageway from Darwin CBD to Howard Springs; rest sealed (fully sealed Feb 1987). Single carriageway with road trains outside the Darwin area is from general sources; the page "road type" detail for the full route is NOT CONFIRMED beyond that.
- No genuine alternative sealed route between the two ends. Detours only: Kakadu (via Arnhem Highway at about km 40 per Wikipedia), Nitmiluk (Katherine Gorge) from Katherine, Litchfield (see darwin-kakadu-litchfield-loop). Keep to one line each.
- Mataranka to Katherine is 106 km and Mataranka thermal pools are 120 km south of Katherine near Mataranka (NT Parks search extract: "Elsey National Park ... about 120 km south of Katherine").

## 2. Legs and overnight stops (OSRM times, no stops)
OSRM per leg: Darwin-Adelaide River 112 km 1h20; Adelaide River-Katherine 204 km 2h09; Katherine-Mataranka 106 km 1h07; Mataranka-Daly Waters 168 km 1h43; Daly Waters-Elliott 155 km 1h40; Elliott-Tennant Creek 254 km 2h34; Tennant Creek-Devils Marbles 104 km 1h03; Devils Marbles-Barrow Creek 140 km 1h57; Barrow Creek-Alice Springs 282 km 2h50.
Suggested four-day split (OSRM): Day 1 Darwin to Katherine 316 km (3h29). Day 2 Katherine to Daly Waters 274 km (2h50), or stop at Mataranka (106 km). Day 3 Daly Waters to Tennant Creek 409 km (4h14). Day 4 Tennant Creek to Alice Springs 526 km (5h50, via Devils Marbles).
AussieCamps campground data holds no campgrounds within 15 km of this route (corridor tool: "0 places"), so say nothing from the app about caravan parks. Named places below are from official tourism or NT Parks pages.
- **Darwin (start)**: see darwin-kakadu-litchfield-loop. Dump point in data: Darwin Greyhound Club Dump Point, Winnellie (AU16115, free, no washout tap listed).
- **Adelaide River (112 km)**: Dump point in data: Adelaide River Dump Point (AU18414, free). Also on the corridor: Coomalie Creek RV Park dump point (AU18417, km 87), Batchelor (AU13709, washout tap, Toilets, Accessible toilet; 8.5 km off route) and Batchelor Dump Point (AU18416).
- **Katherine (about 316 km)**: base for Nitmiluk (Katherine Gorge). Dump points in data: Caravan Dump Point (AU16326, washout tap) and Katherine Caravan Dump Point (AU16117, 2.7 km off). Named caravan parks: NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Mataranka (about 422 km)**: thermal pool and Bitter Springs in Elsey National Park (NT Parks search extract: thermal waters hold a steady 34 C year round; NT Parks Visitor Pass needed, bought before arriving, NT residents exempt; park contact (08) 8973 8888). A tourism page says Bitter Springs closes in the wet season (typically November to May) because of flooding (search extract, not NT Government): check on the day. Dump point: Mataranka Dump Point (AU18419).
- **Daly Waters (about 590 km from Darwin per Wikipedia)**: Tourism NT itinerary describes the Daly Waters Historic Pub as a place to eat, drink and stay, with camping, powered sites, safari tents and cabins (search extract of a tourism itinerary; the Tourism NT Explorers Way page lists it as the Day 10 overnight). Carpentaria Highway to Borroloola starts here (Wikipedia: Daly Waters, Carpentaria Highway at about km 590).
- **Tennant Creek (about 965 km at Threeways per Wikipedia; Threeways is 25 km north of town)**: town is the usual overnight. Named parks: NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Devils Marbles / Karlu Karlu (about 96 km south of Tennant Creek, per northernterritory.com)**: Tourism NT page: "Entry into Karlu Karlu requires an NT Parks Visitor Pass"; this is a separate pass to Kakadu and Uluru-Kata Tjuta passes. Camping: "you must book online and pay a fee to camp ... you won't be able to pay at the site" (Tourism NT page and its extract). The page says there is a bush camping area with fireplaces and free Wi-Fi. A search extract of a third-party listing says 24 sites with no power, water or pets: NOT CONFIRMED by an official source. Pass prices from a Tourism NT table in a search extract: 1-day adult $10, child $5, concession $8, family $25 (NOT CONFIRMED on the booking site; do not print as current).
- **Barrow Creek, Ti Tree, Aileron**: services NOT CONFIRMED. Wikipedia says fuel and other facilities are usually available "about every 200 km".
- **Alice Springs (about 1,499 km, arrival)**: Dump points in data: Alice Springs Dump Point (AU16113, free, 4.4 km off), Olive Pink Botanic Gardens (AU15224, 1.1 km off; Drinking water, Toilets, Accessible toilet), Alice Springs Showground, Ilparpa (AU18415, 5.1 km off). ## 3. Fuel and services
- Tourism NT (roads safety page, fetched): "When travelling to remote parts of the NT, you should use a vehicle with a long range fuel tank and take extra fuel." Remote stations have limited hours.
- Wikipedia (secondary): fuel and facilities usually about every 200 km. Rita's Outback Guide campervan page (search extract, secondary): distances between supplies north of Alice Springs are "never greater than 140 km".
- Named roadhouse-to-roadhouse longest gap from Tourism NT or NT Government: NOT CONFIRMED. Do not name a longest gap on this route.
- Fuel point named in NT sources: Wauchope (9 km south of Devils Marbles, "fuel and limited supplies": NT Government Karlu Karlu fact sheet, search extract). Wycliffe Well is 130 km south of Tennant Creek and 380 km north of Alice Springs (Tourism NT page, search extract).
- The Barkly Highway junction (Threeways Roadhouse) is 25 km north of Tennant Creek (search extract).

## 4. Road conditions and seasonal closures
- Live source: Road Report NT, roadreport.nt.gov.au or 1800 246 199 (Tourism NT roads page, quoted: "www.roadreport.nt.gov.au or by phone on 1800 246 199").
- Wet season: Tourism NT: from November to April "some roads may be closed due to flooding"; do not drive on closed roads (fines can be heavy); conditions can change quickly, check daily. NT Government flood study (search extract): Stuart Highway closed 100 times between October 2005 and March 2024, closures averaging about 35 hours. Past closures reported by local news, historical only: north of Threeways and between Tennant Creek and Threeways. Link wet-season-camping-top-end-northern-wa.
- Speed limits (NT Traffic Regulations extract, search): default outside a built-up area is 110 km/h, but "a speed-limit of 130 kilometres per hour applies ... where that speed is indicated by speed-limit signs". NT Government page (search extract): "some sections of the following highways has a maximum speed of 130km/h as indicated": Barkly, Stuart, Victoria and Arnhem. Tourism NT: "The open-road limit is 110 km/h unless signed otherwise, and certain highways allow up to 130 km/h." Speed cameras are used across the NT. Wikipedia: open limit trial 2014 to 2016 ended in November 2016. A Department of Logistics and Infrastructure page lists a trial of 130 km/h on sections of the Stuart Highway posted at 100 km/h by Hayes Creek and Wycliffe Well (undated search extract): do not state as current. Safe wording: "up to 130 km/h where signed; the sign decides".
- Road trains (NT Government, search extract): can be over 50 m long with up to three trailers; overtaking can take up to 2.5 km at 100 km/h; need at least 1 km of clear road ahead; at 100 km/h a car needs about 157 m to stop and a truck about 194 m; do not drive in convoys, especially when towing. Tourism NT: when one approaches on a narrow or unsealed road, ease onto the shoulder. Link driving-with-road-trains-australia.
- Wildlife (Tourism NT): many NT roads are unfenced; animals appear especially dawn, dusk and night; "Never swerve to avoid animals" (slow down and sound the horn).
- Tolls: none on this route (not sourced; do not mention).
- Roadworks: none confirmed for 21 October 2026.

## 5. Border and biosecurity rules
- The NT has no state border checkpoint on this route that we found. SBS (third-party, search extract) says NT borders are not patrolled and there are quarantine bins at airports and railway stations. Tourism NT pages did not cover it.
- Interstate Quarantine (interstatequarantine.org.au, page undated, opened 2026-10-21) quotes the NT:
  - "The Ti Tree Fruit Fly Exclusion Zone extends from Tennant Creek to Alice Springs. Do not take fruit (including capsicum, chilli, tomato, eggplant, cucumber, zucchini and squash) into this zone." This applies to a Darwin to Alice Springs drive from Tennant Creek south.
  - "Restrictions apply to fruit and vegetables when travelling south from Darwin to prevent the spread of the pest melon thrips. The following produce must not be carried south along the Stuart Highway beyond the Adelaide River township: beans, capsicum, chilli, cucumber, eggplant, melons, mango fruit and mango plants (unless treated against leaf hopper), okra, pumpkin, silverbeet, squash, tomato and zucchini."
- NT Government (search extract of nt.gov.au plants-and-quarantine pages, 403 to script): "If you are travelling to the township of Ti Tree, north of Alice Springs, you must dispose of any fruit you have brought from interstate"; quarantine bins at Alice Springs Airport and railway station; unsure items: check the Interstate Quarantine site or contact Plant Biosecurity. Writer: this contradicts the idea that "nothing applies". Say exactly: southbound from Darwin you must not carry the listed produce beyond Adelaide River, and do not take fruit into the Tennant Creek to Alice Springs zone.
- Also in the extract: uncertified bananas from North Queensland are not allowed into the NT; mango varieties not needing treatment are Kensington Pride, Calypso, Honey Gold. Not relevant to a Darwin start (Darwin to Alice) except for what you buy before. Mention only a link: interstate-biosecurity-road-trip.
- Date opened of any zone: NOT CONFIRMED.

## 7. Other facts a driver needs
- Time zone: the NT is on Australian Central Standard Time (ACST, UTC+9:30) and does not observe daylight saving (timeanddate and other secondary pages, search extract; no NT Government page opened). No time change on this route. Say "the NT does not use daylight saving".
- Park passes: NT Parks Visitor Pass for Karlu Karlu and for Elsey National Park (Tourism NT pages), NT residents exempt. Tell readers to buy it before the trip online.
- Crocodiles (NT Government Be Crocwise, search extract): swim only where a swimming area sign is displayed; "If a waterway does not have a swimming area sign, do not swim in this area"; the government's swimming table lists Mataranka thermal pool and Bitter Springs as open all year except during extreme flooding; Katherine Gorge above the first gorge is dry season only; "The Cascades" listed as currently closed. Camp at least 50 m from the water's edge; land-based anglers 5 m back. Crocodile management team to report: 0419 822 859. Page status can change: tell readers to check.
- Kakadu and Nitmiluk: optional detours, not researched further; Kakadu is under Parks Australia, not NT Government.
