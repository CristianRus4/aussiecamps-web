# Italian style guide (it-IT) for AussieCamps and KiwiCamping

This file is the contract for every Italian unit on aussiecamps.app and campingapp.nz. The same
file lives in both repos. Whoever writes or judges Italian copy for either site follows it.

Reader: an Italian aged roughly 20 to 40 planning a road trip, a camper rental or a Working Holiday
year in Australia or New Zealand. Many are on a tight budget, many travel in a van or a rented
camper, some come with a tent and a hire car. They read on a phone, often while already travelling.

The test for every sentence: *would an Italian travel writer who has done this trip write it this
way for an Italian reader?* If a native reader would notice it was translated, rewrite it.

## 1. Voice

Write like an experienced Italian who has driven these roads and is telling a friend what actually
matters: concrete, calm, a bit dry, never breathless. Think of a good piece in *Lonely Planet
Italia*, *PleinAir* or a seasoned Italian travel blog, not a brochure and not a legal notice.

- **Address the reader with *tu*** (configured register `informal`). Imperatives in the *tu* form:
  *controlla*, *prenota*, *porta*, *fai il pieno*. Never *Lei*, never *voi* for the reader (if the
  English means a group: *se viaggi in due*, *se siete in quattro nel van* only when the subject is
  the group itself).
- **Drop subject pronouns and possessives Italian does not need.** Not *Tu puoi controllare il tuo
  telefono*, but *Puoi controllare il telefono*. Not *metti il tuo cibo nel tuo frigo*, but *metti il
  cibo in frigo*.
- **Rhythm.** Italian sentences can be a little longer than English ones and use connectives
  (*quindi*, *però*, *invece*, *infatti*, *anche se*, *tanto vale*). Merge two choppy English
  sentences when Italian would; split a long English sentence when it reads better. Do this only
  within a paragraph, never across paragraphs, tips or FAQ items.
- **Active and concrete.** Prefer an active verb or the impersonal *si* to *viene/vengono* + past
  participle. Prefer a verb to an abstract noun: *prenota in anticipo* rather than *effettua una
  prenotazione anticipata*.
- **Put the important thing first.** Italian readers like the point up front: *A gennaio i rifugi
  sono pieni: parti con la tenda* rather than translating an English build-up word by word.
- **Tips** (the short bullet lines) are compact phrases without a final full stop, as in English:
  *Fai il pieno prima del tratto lungo*, *Le Great Walks vanno prenotate mesi prima*.
- **Headings and titles use sentence case** (only the first word and proper names capitalised):
  *Regole del campeggio nel New South Wales*, never *Regole Del Campeggio*.
- **No marketing gush.** No *incredibile*, *mozzafiato*, *il viaggio dei tuoi sogni*, *l’avventura
  ti aspetta* unless the English says exactly that. Warm, yes; inflated, no.
- **Safety copy** is calm and direct, never alarmist: *Se il guado è in piena, aspetta* rather than
  *Attenzione! Pericolo di morte!*.

## 2. Glossary (English term -> Italian, with evidence)

pair *campeggio* with the English state name, e.g. "northern territory campeggio", and on

## 3. Conventions

- **Numbers:** thousands with a full stop, decimals with a comma: *74.000*, *2,08*, *1.500 km*.
  Four-digit years and model numbers have no separator (*2026*).
- **Currency:** prices stay in AUD (AussieCamps) or NZD (KiwiCamping). Keep the symbol the English
  uses and put it after the number with a space: *80 $*, *2,08 A$*, *110 NZ$*, *da 80 a 130 $*.
  Keep the codes *AUD*/*NZD* where the English uses them. Never convert to euro unless the English
  does. In prose *dollari australiani* / *dollari neozelandesi* is fine when no figure follows.
- **Ranges:** *da 80 a 130 $*, or *80-130 $*; *10-15%* (no space before %).
- **Units:** metric as in the English: *km*, *km/h*, *m*, *kg*, *l* or *litri*, *°C*. *2,08 A$/l*.
- **Dates:** *5 agosto 2026*, *1º luglio*, months and weekdays lowercase. *da ottobre ad aprile*.
  Seasons are the southern-hemisphere ones; when the English says "summer", it is *l’estate
  australe* only if the contrast matters to an Italian reader (*a gennaio, in piena estate*).
- **Time:** 24-hour clock: *alle 20*, *dalle 8 alle 17*.
- **Quotation marks:** «caporali» for quotations and sign text: *il cartello «No camping»*.
  Typographic apostrophe **’** everywhere (*l’app*, *un’area*, *dell’Australia*), except inside proper
  names copied from the source (keep *K'gari* exactly as the English writes it).
- **Dashes:** no em dashes, ever. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or a new sentence. Hyphen for
  ranges as above.
- **Punctuation:** no space before *: ; ! ?*; lowercase after a colon unless a proper noun follows.
  No Oxford comma (*bagni, docce e acqua*). Avoid exclamation marks except in a genuine warning.
- **Capitals:** *Paese* (the country) vs *paese* (village/small town); *Stato* only for the state
  as an institution, *stato* for the federal states in running text (*stato per stato*). Seasons,
  months, days and nationalities lowercase (*gli australiani*, *i neozelandesi*, *i Māori* is the
  exception: capitalised, invariable).
- **Place names:** Australian state and territory names stay in English (the engine protects
  them) with the article Italians use: *nel New South Wales*, *nel Queensland*, *in Victoria* /
  *nello stato di Victoria*, *in Tasmania*, *nel Western Australia*, *nel South Australia*, *nel
  Northern Territory*, *nell’ACT*. On a page about one state, you may add the Italian exonym once in
  brackets where one is common (*New South Wales (Nuovo Galles del Sud)*, *Western Australia
  (Australia Occidentale)*, *South Australia (Australia Meridionale)*, *Northern Territory
  (Territorio del Nord)*). Countries use Italian: *Australia*, *Nuova Zelanda*. Cities keep their
  names (*Sydney*, *Christchurch*). Common Italian exonyms are allowed: *Stretto di Cook*, *Mar di
  Tasman*. On KiwiCamping, *North Island*, *South Island* and *Stewart Island* are protected terms:
  write *Isola del Nord (North Island)* / *Isola del Sud (South Island)* the first time in a unit and
  *Isola del Nord/Sud* is then natural; Stewart Island stays *Stewart Island (Rakiura)*.
- **Māori and Aboriginal names:** exact spelling and macrons always (*Māori*, *Taupō*, *Ōhakune*,
  *Whakatāne*, *Aotearoa*, *Aoraki*, *Uluṟu*, *K'gari*, *Ikara*, *Tjoritja*). They never take a plural
  *-s* or an Italian ending. *Country* (Aboriginal sense) = *il Country, la terra ancestrale dei
  popoli aborigeni* on first use.
- **Loanwords** are invariable and keep Italian gender: *il camper / i camper*, *il van / i van*,
  *il caravan park / i caravan park*, *l’holiday park / gli holiday park*, *il roadhouse*, *il
  widget*, *la sandfly / le sandfly*, *la Great Walk / le Great Walks* (proper name of the network).
- **Agency names:** keep them, gloss the first time on each page; never invent an Italian name.
- **Facts:** every number, date, price, distance, legal limit, link and source label survives
  exactly. Source labels (`sources[].label`) are translated as descriptive Italian
  (*Numbeo: fasce di prezzo in Australia, aggiornate a giugno 2026*) but the publisher name stays.

## 4. Banned

### Never write

- Em dashes (—). The audit rejects them.
- Any "offline map" claim: **mappa offline**, **mappe offline**, *mappa senza connessione*,
  *navigazione offline* said of AussieCamps or KiwiCamping. The apps keep **place details and
  guides** on the phone; map tiles, routing, weather and photos need a signal. Write *le schede dei
  luoghi e le guide restano consultabili anche senza campo*. When the English gives general advice
  about downloading maps in another app, say *scarica in anticipo le mappe del percorso sul
  telefono*, never *mappe offline*.
- Free access to Pass data. Only BookEasy (AU) or BookIt (NZ) stays and tours are free in the apps.
- Superlatives or promises the English does not make (*il migliore*, *il più completo*, *tutti i
  campeggi*, *sempre aggiornato*).
- English left in prose where Italian has a word: *campground*, *campsite*, *booking*, *fee*,
  *ranger station* (*centro visitatori*), *amenities*.

## 6. Structure rules (the site's completeness gate)

- One Italian value per English unit, same key. Never merge two units into one or leave one empty:
  sections, paragraphs, tips, FAQ questions and answers, sources and price-table rows must stay in
  the same number and order as the English.
- Keep every placeholder (`{name}`), markup tag (`<a data-guide="slug">…</a>`), URL and number exactly.
- Titles and meta descriptions lead with the term Italians search (*campeggio libero*, *camper*,
