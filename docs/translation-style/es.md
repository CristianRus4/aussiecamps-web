# Spanish (es) translation style guide

Shared by AussieCamps (`/es`, Australia) and KiwiCamping (`/es`, New Zealand). This file is
identical in both repositories. Every Spanish unit on both sites follows it; judges score against it.

Engine locale: `es` (written variety es-ES). Register: informal **tú** (`docs/translation.config.json`).

## 1. Who we write for and how we sound

**Readers.** Two groups read the same page:

- Travellers from Spain planning a campervan or motorhome trip (holiday of 2 to 6 weeks).
- Latin American working-holiday travellers (Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, Mexico, also Colombia and
  Peru): 18 to 35, often living in a van for months, on a budget, picking up farm or hospitality work.

So we write **neutral, natural Spanish with a Spain base** (spelling, accents, numbers), but we avoid
words that only one side uses when a shared word exists (see the vocabulary rules below). A reader in
Madrid and a reader in Santiago should both feel it was written for them.

**Voice.** A Spanish-speaking travel writer who has driven these roads, writing for a friend:

- Direct and practical. Lead with what the reader has to do or decide. Concrete verbs over abstract
  nouns: «Reserva antes de diciembre», not «Se recomienda la realización de la reserva».
- Calm and honest. Safety-aware without drama. No hype, no superlatives the English does not support.
- Sentence rhythm: medium-length sentences that flow, broken by a short one when it matters. Spanish
  tolerates slightly longer sentences than English, but not chains of three subordinate clauses.
  Merge two choppy English sentences into one when Spanish reads better; split a long one.
- Address: one reader, **tú**, always. Imperatives in tú (elige, reserva, mira, ten en cuenta).
  **Never** vosotros/os/vuestro (alienates Latin America), never ustedes or usted (reads formal or
  regional). Use impersonal «se» only for general rules («En los parques nacionales no se puede
  hacer fuego»), not as a way to avoid addressing the reader.
- Do not open every paragraph with the reader. Vary: start with the place, the rule, the fact.

**Register checks.** One «tú» voice across the page. No «Le recomendamos», no «su vehículo».

### Names that never change

- Brands: AussieCamps, KiwiCamping, BookEasy, BookIt, App Store, Street View, Apple.
- Agencies keep their English name with a short gloss on first use per page, then the acronym:
  «el DOC (Department of Conservation, el organismo público que gestiona los parques y espacios
  protegidos de Nueva Zelanda)», «NPWS (el servicio de parques nacionales de New South Wales)»,
  «Parks Victoria», «Waka Kotahi», «MPI», «Home Affairs», «Immigration New Zealand».
- Protected place names (the engine rejects anything else): the Australian states and territories
  **New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania, Northern
  Territory, Australian Capital Territory, Central Australia**, and NZ's **North Island, South
  Island, Stewart Island, Aotearoa, Aoraki**. Use them with the Spanish article and gender of the
  Spanish noun: «en New South Wales», «el Northern Territory», «la North Island», «la South
  Island». Where the page is about that state or island, add the Spanish exonym once in the
  intro or first mention: «New South Wales (Nueva Gales del Sur)», «Western Australia (Australia
  Occidental)», «South Australia (Australia Meridional)», «Northern Territory (Territorio del
  Norte)», «la North Island (Isla Norte)», «la South Island (Isla Sur)». Adjectives are not protected:
  «los parques de Australia Occidental» is fine where the English says «Western Australian parks».
- Other place names stay as on the map and the signs (Great Ocean Road, Cape York, Bay of Islands,
  West Coast, Coromandel, Milford Sound / Piopiotahi). Established Spanish exonyms are fine where
  they exist: **Australia, Nueva Zelanda, Sídney, Tasmania, la Gran Barrera de Coral** («Great
  Barrier Reef» stays if the English names the marine park authority).
- Māori and Aboriginal names keep exact spelling, macrons and diacritics: Uluṟu, K'gari, Ikara,
  Tjoritja, Aoraki, Tāmaki Makaurau, Whanganui, Ōtautahi. Words like whenua, Country (Aboriginal
  sense) keep their meaning: «respetar el whenua (la tierra, en sentido maorí)», «respetar Country
  (la tierra y su significado para los pueblos aborígenes)».
- URLs, `{placeholders}`, markup (`<a data-guide="slug">…</a>`) and numbers are copied exactly.

## 3. Conventions

- **Numbers:** decimal comma, thousands point: «2,08», «74.000», «6.500». Years without a point
  (2026). Keep «74.000+» style in stat tiles where the English uses «74,000+».
- **Currency:** amounts stay in AUD / NZD. Write the code after the number with a space: «35 AUD»,
  «2,96 NZD por litro», ranges «entre 80 y 140 AUD» in prose or «80–140 AUD» in tables and labels.
  Where the English writes a bare «$25», write «25 NZD» / «25 AUD»: for a Latin American reader a bare
  «$» means pesos. Cents of a dollar are **centavos**, never «céntimos».
- **Percent:** «15 %» (space, per RAE/Fundéu).
- **Dates:** «19 de octubre de 2026», months and weekdays lowercase. «de diciembre a febrero».
- **Times:** 24-hour «18:00» or «a las 6 de la tarde» in running prose.
- **Units:** km, km/h, m, °C, l (litros; «litros» spelled out in prose), kg. «l/100 km».
- **Punctuation:** opening ¿ and ¡ always. Quotation marks « » (inner “ ”). In-app labels in « »
  when quoted inside a sentence: toca «Añadir al viaje». Spanish sentence case in titles and headings:
  only the first word and proper nouns get a capital («Normas de acampada en Queensland», never
  «Normas De Acampada»). No serial comma before «y».
- **Dashes:** no em dash (—) anywhere. No en dash (–) as a sentence dash either; use a comma, colon,
  parentheses or a full stop. «–» is allowed only inside number ranges in tables and labels.
- **Abbreviations:** «p. ej.» sparingly; prefer «por ejemplo».

## 4. Titles, H1s and meta descriptions

- Lead with the term a Spanish speaker searches: «Acampada libre en Nueva Zelanda: …»,
  «Nueva Zelanda en autocaravana: …», «Normas de acampada en Queensland», «¿Se puede dormir en el
  coche en Australia?», «Puntos de vaciado en Australia», «Cuánto cuesta…», «Precios de…».
- Natural length: titles up to ~65 characters, meta descriptions ~150 characters. Do not pad.
- Questions in titles keep the question form when the English is a question.
- Year stays where the English has it («… en 2026»).

### Lessons from the first judge round (2026-10-19)

- In-app list and button names always go in « » inside a sentence: «Me gustó», «Quiero visitar»,
  «Opciones de privacidad». Refer to the smart lists as «las listas «Me gustó»…» so «solas» agrees.
- Never put «tiempo real» next to «el tiempo» (weather). Use «en línea» or «online» for live data
  and «previsión del tiempo» for the forecast.
- Meta descriptions near 150 characters; drop secondary items before going over.
- «mejor de como», not «mejor que como». «Crea» (neutral) rather than «Arma» (Latin America) or
  «Monta» (Spain) for building a route or list.
- Do not calque playful English: «No mystery maths» is «Sin cuentas raras», not «Sin cuentas
  misteriosas».
- «básicos» is not a noun for «essentials»: «imprescindibles».
- Keep impersonal infinitive lists consistent: «Acampar…, gestionar…, respetar…», not a tú
  reflexive in the middle («deshacerte»).

## 7. Structure rules (the site enforces them)

- Same number of sections, paragraphs, tips, FAQ items, sources and price rows as the English. You
  may reorder, merge or split sentences **inside** one paragraph only.
- Every fact, number, date, price, distance and link survives. If the English is wrong, translate it
  faithfully and report it; do not silently fix facts.
- Tips are short labels (2 to 7 words), not sentences with a full stop unless the English has one.
- Homepage testimonials (`review1`…`review9*`, `reviewsEyebrow`, `reviewsTitle`) are never rewritten.
