# Norwegian Bokmål (nb) style guide for AussieCamps and KiwiCamping

This is the contract for every Norwegian unit on aussiecamps.app/nb and campingapp.nz/nb. The same
file lives in both repositories. Write Norwegian that reads as if a Norwegian travel writer who has
driven a bobil around Australia and New Zealand wrote it for readers in Norway. Nobody should be able
to tell that an English page came first.

## 1. Reader and voice

**Who reads this.** Norwegians planning a bobil or campervan trip, often three to six weeks in the
southern summer; couples and families who have camped in Norway and Europe; young Norwegians on a
working holiday visa who buy an old van or sleep in the car; and retired travellers with time for a
long loop. They know Norwegian camping well: campingplass, bobilparkering, tømmestasjon, rasteplass,
allemannsretten, bålforbud, EU-kontroll, AutoPASS. They do not know Australian or New Zealand systems
(councils, DOC, freedom camping, self-contained certification, Total Fire Ban, WOF, rego), so those get
a short Norwegian gloss the first time they appear on a page.

**Register: du**, lower case (du, deg, din), as set in `docs/translation.config.json`. Every
Norwegian travel and camping publisher addresses the reader this way; De/Dem is archaic and banned.
Use generic **man** only where Norwegian naturally prefers it in neutral rule statements ("Man kjører
på venstre side"), and keep advice to the reader in du.

**Rhythm and tone.** Calm, concrete, practical, slightly understated, like an experienced friend.
Norwegian readers distrust hype: no "magisk", "helt fantastisk", "en opplevelse for livet" unless the
English says something equally strong. Short and medium sentences; split an English sentence when it
piles up clauses, merge two short ones when Norwegian reads choppy. Put the information the reader
acts on first. Within a paragraph, sentences may be reordered, split or merged.

**Verbs, not nouns.** English nominal phrases tempt Norwegian into kansellispråk ("foreta en
bestilling", "gjennomføring av turen", "med hensyn til"). Write "bestille", "når du kjører turen",
"om".

**Fewer possessives.** English says "your car, your tyres, your booking"; Norwegian drops most of
them: "Sjekk dekkene før du kjører", not "Sjekk dekkene dine på bilen din". When a possessive is
needed it goes after the noun ("bilen din"); "din bil" is emphatic and sounds translated.

**Present tense, not "vil".** English "will" is usually plain present in Norwegian: "Du trenger
førerkort", not "Du vil trenge førerkort". "Vil" means want.

**Imperatives.** Body text and tips use the imperative ("Sjekk skiltene", "Bestill i god tid", "Fyll
vann før du forlater byen"). Tips may also be short noun phrases where that is tighter ("Plass med
strøm fra rundt 50 AUD").

**Headings** use sentence case (only the first word and names capitalised), no full stop. They may
be a short statement or a question, whatever a Norwegian magazine would print.

**Titles, H1 and meta.** Lead with the words Norwegians actually type (section 4). Titles under about
60 characters, meta descriptions about 140 to 160. Be concrete: "Campingregler i Victoria: booking,
gratis camping og bålforbud" beats an echo of the English.

**Spelling: moderate Bokmål** as in NRK and Aftenposten. Masculine -en endings for most feminine
nouns (natten, uken, turen, avgiften) but **hytta** and **øya** (Nordøya, Sørøya), which dominate in
everyday writing. Past tense -et (kastet, regnet). Choose and keep: **vei**, **bru** (as on Statens
vegvesen signs: enfeltsbru), **ferje**, **frem**, **slik** (not sånn), **ennå** (yet), **etter
hvert**.

## 3. Conventions

- **Numbers:** space as thousands separator, comma for decimals: 74 000, 6 500, 2,08. Use a
  non-breaking space (U+00A0) inside numbers so they never wrap. "74,000+" becomes "over 74 000" in
  prose and "74 000+" in stat tiles. Four-digit numbers also take the space (4 000) except years.
  Keep every number exactly; never round or convert.
- **Currency:** amount, space, ISO code: "35 AUD", "2,08 AUD per liter", "15 NZD". "A$", "AU$" and "$"
  in an Australian context become AUD; "NZ$" and "$" in a New Zealand context become NZD. "australske
  dollar" / "newzealandske dollar" are fine once in running prose. Never convert to kroner. Ranges:
  "80–130 AUD" in tables and tips (en dash, no spaces), "80 til 130 AUD" in prose.
- **Dates and times:** "5. august 2026", "1. juni", "fra mai til september"; months and weekdays lower
  case. Times with a full stop: "kl. 21.00", "kl. 22.00–07.00", "innen kl. 10". Seasons are the
  southern ones: add "(desember til februar)" only where the English names a season and the month
  matters to a reader in Norway.
- **Units:** km, km/t, m, °C, liter (l in tables: l/100 km). Never convert to mil.
- **Percent:** "15 prosent" in prose, "15 %" in tables and tips.
- **Quotes:** «…», nested ‘…’. App labels and sign wording go in «…».
- **Punctuation:** Norwegian comma rules: comma after a subordinate clause that opens the sentence
  ("Når du kommer frem, ..."), before men, and between two main clauses joined by og when each has
  its own subject. No comma before og in lists. After a colon, lower case unless a full sentence
  follows. **No em dashes (—).** Do not use a spaced en dash ( – ) as a tankestrek either; use a
  comma, colon, parentheses or a new sentence. The en dash is only for ranges.
- **Compounds are closed** (særskriving is the most visible translation error in Norwegian):
  campingplass, bobilparkering, drivstoffpris, nasjonalparkavgift, plassavgift. Hyphen after
  abbreviations, numbers and brands: DOC-hytte, 4WD-vei, iPhone-app, 24-timersregel. Multi-word English
  terms join with a hyphen before the Norwegian part: freedom camping-plass, working holiday-visum,
  Great Walk-hytte.
- **Capitals:** nationality adjectives and inhabitants are lower case: australsk, newzealandsk,
  australiere, newzealendere. Months, weekdays and seasons lower case.
- **Place names:** keep the English or official form: Great Ocean Road, Gold Coast, Blue Mountains,
  Milford Sound, Bay of Islands, Cape Reinga, Great Barrier Reef. Keep Aboriginal and Māori names
  exactly, with diacritics and macrons: Uluṟu, K'gari, Ikara, Tjoritja, Aotearoa, Aoraki, Wānaka,
  Whakatāne, Rakiura, Māori, te reo. Norwegian exonyms: **Australia**, **New Zealand**, **Tasmania**
  (all unchanged), **Nordøya**, **Sørøya** (SNL, world-wide-wheels), **Cookstredet**,
  **Tasmanhavet**. Stewart Island stays (or Rakiura).
- **Prepositions:** **i Australia**, **på New Zealand** (the established Norwegian form, benns.no),
  **på Tasmania**, **på Nordøya / Sørøya**, **på Stewart Island**, **i Queensland**, **i New South
  Wales**, **i outbacken**, **langs Great Ocean Road**.
- **States and territories:** New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western
  Australia, Northern Territory, Australian Capital Territory and Tasmania stay as written, the way
  they appear on signs, maps and park websites (house rule). Do not use Vest-Australia or
  Sør-Australia. Abbreviations NSW, QLD, WA, SA, NT, ACT, VIC, TAS stay.
- **Gender and inflection:** en campingplass, en bobil, en campingvogn, en feriepark, en caravanpark,
  en rasteplass, en tømmestasjon, en hytte (definite hytta), et førerkort, et vogntog, en roadtrip,
  appen. Brand and product names stay uninflected: "med Pass", "i AussieCamps", "DOC-hyttene" (hyphen
  carries the ending). Numbers: **sju** (not syv).
- **Never change:** AussieCamps, KiwiCamping, App Store, iPhone, iOS, BookEasy, BookIt, product and
  park names, agency names and abbreviations (DOC, NPWS, MBIE, NZTA, Waka Kotahi, RBA, RBNZ), URLs,
  `<a data-guide="…">…</a>` markup and `{placeholders}`. Translate the link text inside markup and keep
  the tag and its attributes byte for byte. Avoid genitive forms of the brands ("AussieCamps'"): write
  "i AussieCamps", "appen KiwiCamping".
- **Structure:** keep the same number of sections, paragraphs, tips, FAQ items, sources, table rows
  and price rows as English. Sentences may move, split or merge inside one paragraph only.
- **Norwegian analogies** (allemannsretten, bålforbud, EU-kontroll, AutoPASS, DNT, knott, prikker)
  are allowed as a short gloss, once per page, only where they are accurate. They never replace the
  local fact and never add a new rule, number or claim.

## 5. Banned

- **Any offline-map claim:** "offline-kart", "offlinekart", "kart uten nett", "kartet fungerer uten
  dekning", "offline campingkart" (also as a meta keyword). The apps are not offline maps. Write what is
  true: "Stedsinformasjonen og guidene ligger i appen og fungerer uten dekning. Kartfliser, ruteplanlegging,
  vær og bilder trenger nett."
- **De, Dem, Deres** as forms of address.
- **Em dashes**, and spaced en dashes used as dashes.
- **Superlatives the English does not make** ("magisk", "fantastisk", "helt unik", "verdens beste",
  "en opplevelse for livet"). Match the English level.
- **Særskriving:** "camping plass", "bobil parkering", "drivstoff pris", "Caravan parker".
